## Summary

Authors Emanuele Mezzi, Fabio Massacci, and Katja Tuma (arXiv preprint, cs.CR,
2503.23175) present an evaluation methodology for testing state-of-the-art LLMs on
real-size (not toy/snippet) CTI report tasks. They test three LLMs across zero-shot,
few-shot, and fine-tuning paradigms, and explicitly measure two properties beyond raw
accuracy: **consistency** (does the model give the same answer to the same/paraphrased
input across repeated runs) and **confidence calibration** (does the model's stated
confidence track its actual correctness). The central claim of the paper is that this
combination of properties — not just point accuracy — is what makes current LLMs
unsuitable for production CTI use, because inconsistent/overconfident systems degrade
analyst trust and cannot be safely automated without a human in the loop.

## Key claims

1. "LLMs cannot guarantee sufficient performance on real-size reports while also being
   inconsistent and overconfident" — i.e., the failure mode is not purely accuracy but
   a combination of unreliability across repeated queries and miscalibrated confidence,
   evaluated on 350 real-world threat intelligence reports.
2. Few-shot learning and fine-tuning "only partially improve the results," suggesting
   the unreliability is not simply a data/prompting problem that scales away with more
   examples — it persists across all three tested learning paradigms.
3. The paper frames reliability for CTI as a multi-dimensional property (accuracy +
   consistency + calibration), which is a stronger bar than benchmarks that report only
   task accuracy.

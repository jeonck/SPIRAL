# Calibration Without Comprehension (arXiv 2606.20502)

## Why this source was accepted, and what it is NOT

Collected at cycle 120 (a T1 targeting `consistency-calibration-as-failure-mode`) against that
issue's **`open_questions[3]`** — *does the fine-tuning-can-WORSEN-accuracy-and-calibration pattern
observed in src-0001 generalise beyond src-0001's setting?*

**READ THIS BEFORE YOU CITE IT. THE TITLE IS A TRAP.** The word *Calibration* in the title is used
in a **decision-threshold** sense, not a proper-scoring sense. Both renders agree, independently:

> **Expected Calibration Error: ABSENT. Brier score: ABSENT. Reliability diagrams: ABSENT.**

This source therefore **does NOT answer `open_questions[5]`** (the second-CTI-task-ECE/Brier
question) and **must never be proposed for it**. It joins CTIArena, the FIRST 2025 Mezzi PDF and
src-0031 on the do-not-re-propose list *for that question specifically*. It is also **not a CTI
task**: cyber-threat-intelligence, IOC-extraction, MITRE ATT&CK and threat-report tasks are all
**ABSENT** (checked explicitly, both renders). Under the standing exclusion rule it may **not** be
counted toward **CTI** multi-source support for any issue.

What it *is*: a large, security-domain, before-versus-after fine-tuning study whose bearing on this
base is on the **accuracy leg** of the src-0001 fine-tuning pattern, not the calibration leg.

## What the study does

- **Tasks (3):** non-targeted vulnerability detection; targeted vulnerability detection; CWE
  classification / CWE-1000 ranking.
- **Evaluation dataset — CWE-Trace:** '834 context-aware vulnerable–patched samples from 417 Linux
  kernel CVEs', split into **PBD** (≤2024, 628 samples) and **LFD** (2025, 206 samples).
- **Fine-tuning datasets (5):** Devign, LineVul, PrimeVul, MegaVul, VDISC. **Method: LoRA.**
- **Models — 8 vanilla:** CodeLlama (7B), StarCoder2 (7B), Qwen3-Coder (30B), Mistral v0.3 (7B),
  Llama3.1 (8B), DeepSeek-R1 (7B), Qwen3 (4B), GPT-4.1-mini.
  **15 fine-tuned:** Qwen3-4B, Llama3.1-8B and DeepSeek-R1-32B each × the 5 datasets above.
- **Metrics reported (verbatim names):** Standard Accuracy (Acc_std), Coverage (Cov), TP/FP/TN/FN,
  **Directional Failure Index (DFI)**, **Contextual Gain Ratio (CGR)**, Top-k Accuracy (Top-1..5),
  Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), Root-Taxonomy Accuracy (RootMicro@1 / RootMacro@1), **Hierarchical
  Distance and Direction (HDD)**.
- **Confidence elicitation: NOT DESCRIBED.** The paper states 'All inference runs use temperature =
  0' but no mechanism for extracting a confidence score is given. Per the elicitation-method
  requirement standing since cycles 62/75, **failure to state an elicitation method is a defect in
  a candidate**, and it is charged here. Abstentions are handled as a triage outcome, not as a
  confidence value.

## Key claims (verbatim-supported)

**KC1 — the paper's own headline, quoted verbatim from the abstract page (render 2):**

> 'Fine-tuning shifts the output threshold without changing the decision policy. This is
> calibration without comprehension: output distributions adapt to training data while the
> underlying security reasoning remains absent.'

**KC2 — fine-tuning is DIRECTION-UNSTABLE on accuracy: it helps enormously in some model×dataset
pairs and is catastrophic in others.** From the body render, binary detection Overall accuracy:

| model | vanilla | fine-tuned | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-4B | 11.9% | LineVul-FT **50.4%** | **+38.5 pp** |
| Qwen3-4B | 11.9% | VDISC-FT 47.1% | +35.2 pp |
| Llama3.1-8B | 50.6% | Devign-FT **1.3%** | **−49.3 pp** |
| Llama3.1-8B | 50.6% | LineVul-FT 44.9% | −5.7 pp |
| DeepSeek-R1-32B | 49.9% | LineVul-FT 48.5% | −1.4 pp |
| DeepSeek-R1-32B | 49.9% | PrimeVul-FT 43.8% | −6.1 pp |

On CWE classification (Root-Micro@1) the same two-sidedness appears: DeepSeek-R1-32B '+9.2 to +25.9
pp on PBD' with fine-tuning, against Llama3.1-8B '−2.4 to −49.0 pp on LFD'.

**KC3 — vanilla ceiling is near chance, and the aggregate hides a directional collapse.** 'The best
vanilla model, CodeLlama, reaches only 52.1% Overall (+2.1 pp above chance)'; the abstract's
matching wording is 'best detection score reaches only 52.1%'. 'near-50% averages mask extreme
directional failure', with **DFI 'ranging from −85.5 to +94.8 pp'** — DeepSeek-R1 answers *paranoid*
('+94.8 pp on PBD, +90.8 pp on LFD') while GPT-4.1-mini answers *skeptical* ('−85.5 pp on PBD,
−89.3 pp on LFD'). 'Across all eight vanilla models, 86.5% of predictions are lateral errors.'

## What this does and does not do for `consistency-calibration-as-failure-mode`

**DOES (partially) serve `open_questions[3]`, on ONE of its two legs.** src-0001's pattern has an
accuracy leg (gpt4o APT-extraction P/R 0.87→0.68 after fine-tuning) and a calibration leg
(CVE-generation ECE 0.15→0.91). This source **corroborates the accuracy leg** in a different
security domain, on different models, with a different tuning method (LoRA), at a much larger
model×dataset grid — fine-tuning **can** sharply degrade task accuracy, up to −49.3 pp. It says
**nothing whatever about the calibration leg**, because it reports no proper score.

**A successor must not over-read this.** src-0001's setting is CTI extraction; this is Linux-kernel
vulnerability detection. The generalisation established is *'fine-tuning degrading accuracy is not
peculiar to src-0001's setup'*, which is weaker than *'src-0001's pattern generalises'*. The
calibration half of `open_questions[3]` remains **unreplicated anywhere**.

## Limitations — charged, not credited

1. **arXiv preprint.** No peer-review venue stated on either render. Weaker provenance than
   src-0001 (ARES 2025 / Springer LNCS 343-364, corroborated across three artefacts).
2. **AUTHOR-COUNT DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN RENDERS, RECORDED AS A DISAGREEMENT AND NOT RESOLVED.** The
   body render (arxiv.org/html) returns **two** authors, *Arastoo Zibaeirad, Marco Vieira*; the
   abstract render (r.jina.ai) returns only **Arastoo Zibaeirad** and reports the rest ABSENT.
   Neither render outranks the other. A cycle needing the author list must re-derive it (dblp /
   OpenAlex are the working bibliographic routes) rather than trusting either reading here.
3. **AN APPARENT INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY ON Top-1 CWE ACCURACY, FLAGGED AND NOT RESOLVED.** The body
   render returns both 'Best vanilla Top-1 on LFD is 14.71% (GPT-4.1-mini)' and 'Exact CWE ranking
   remains below 1.3% Top-1 accuracy'; the abstract render independently confirms only the second
   ('exact CWE ranking remains below 1.3% Top-1 accuracy'). These two strings cannot both describe
   the same quantity. **NO CYCLE MAY CITE EITHER NUMBER** until the paper's own tables have been
   read directly and the referents separated. The 14.71% figure is the more suspect of the two,
   being unconfirmed by the second render.
4. **No confidence elicitation method stated** (see above) — a defect on this base's own standard.
5. **Authors' own external-validity limit:** 'Study focuses on the Linux kernel (C language)'.
   Also: three backbones with **LoRA only**; five fine-tuning datasets, not exhaustive; zero-shot
   prompts only, excluding few-shot, chain-of-thought and retrieval-augmented benefits — note this
   last one is a *direct* disanalogy with src-0001, which tested zero-shot, few-shot AND
   fine-tuning.
6. **Internal-validity limit stated by the authors:** 'Temperature = 0 may amplify dominant output
   modes; non-zero temperatures could reduce directional bias magnitude.' Since this base's
   consistency evidence concerns repeat-query variability, note that **temperature 0 throughout
   means this source measures no run-to-run consistency at all.**
7. **Construct-validity limit stated by the authors:** 'Binary detection and CWE ranking do not
   fully capture internal reasoning'; HDD 'is fully determined by the CWE-1000 taxonomy graph', so
   its results are taxonomy-version-dependent.

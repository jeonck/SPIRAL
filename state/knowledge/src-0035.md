# Summary

The Register's report on the KPMG incident, by **Carly Page**, published **Fri 12 Jun 2026
16:38 UTC** — the same day as the GPTZero investigation (src-0034). Its value to this base is
narrow and high: **it carries KPMG International's response statement in full and verbatim**, and
that statement is what src-0034 lacks.

This is the source that moves **half (B)** of `institutional-incident-real-world-impact` — "how
do institutions respond when caught?" — which `open_questions[3]` and `[5]` record as **stuck at
N=2 comparable cases since cycle 12**, and which cycle 90 explicitly could not advance. **KPMG is
a third recorded response.**

The response's shape is strikingly close to EY Canada's, and closer than any pair the base has
held: *remove the report*, *review the circumstances of publication*, *invoke an organisational
responsible-AI policy*, and **name no cause**. Under `open_questions[5]`'s untested hypothesis —
does volunteering-a-cause versus declining-to predict anything? — **KPMG falls on the EY side**,
giving 2 decline / 1 volunteer rather than 1 / 1. Cycle 99 is a T1 and enters **no candidate
resolution and no score**; the pairing is set out here so that the T2 and any later T3 can price
it, and so that the near-identity of the EY and KPMG wordings is on the record before anyone
treats them as independent confirmations of each other.

**NOTE THE PARTIAL REMEDY, WHICH IS NOT THE SAME AS EY'S.** The Register says the report was
removed **"from some of its websites"** — not withdrawn entirely. `open_questions[6]` records
that cycle 72 narrowed half (B)'s divergence to a **single** surviving dimension, *remedy:
withdraw versus retain-and-patch*. A partial, still-under-investigation removal sits between
those two poles rather than on either, which bears directly on that dimension.

# Key claims

1. **A THIRD RECORDED INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE, VERBATIM: "KPMG International takes the accuracy
   and integrity of its published content seriously. The report has been removed and we are
   reviewing the circumstances surrounding its publication. We expect all our people to follow
   our guidelines on the responsible use of AI, including human oversight to validate content and
   verify independent sources."** This is the first addition to half (B) of the target issue since
   cycle 12. **KPMG names no cause** for the defective citations — it states that it is
   *reviewing* the circumstances, which withholds a cause rather than supplying one.
2. **THE KPMG RESPONSE IS STRUCTURALLY NEAR-IDENTICAL TO EY CANADA'S, INCLUDING ITS CENTRAL
   PHRASE.** EY's quoted sentence (src-0012) is "reviewing the circumstances that led to this
   article's publication"; KPMG's is "reviewing the circumstances surrounding its publication".
   Both pair that with a forward-looking responsible-AI commitment (EY: "organisation-wide
   commitment to the responsible use of AI"; KPMG: "our guidelines on the responsible use of
   AI"). **BOTH DECLINE TO STATE A CAUSE.** ENISA, by contrast, volunteered one (human error; AI
   confined to minor editorial revisions — src-0004, src-0033). This is a **third data point on
   the one surviving divergence dimension**, not a proof of any predictor: two Big Four firms
   responding alike is equally consistent with "commercial exposure predicts the response" and
   with "Big Four crisis-communications boilerplate converges", and this base cannot yet
   distinguish those.
3. **THE REMEDY IS PARTIAL AND EXPLICITLY PROVISIONAL: "KPMG has since removed the report from
   some of its websites while it investigates how the publication made it into the wild."**
   *Some* of its websites, and *while it investigates* — so as of 12 June 2026 the remedy is
   neither EY's full withdrawal nor ENISA's retain-and-patch. Any cycle scoring the
   withdraw-versus-patch dimension must treat this as a third value, not as a repeat of EY's.

# Limitations

- **SINGLE FETCH, ONE RENDER FAMILY.** Cycle 99 fetched this URL once, on the direct render only,
  and did not run the `r.jina.ai` pair-render. The statement's fourth sentence is corroborated
  verbatim by an independent outlet (src-0036, TechCrunch), but **sentences 1–3 of the statement
  rest on this single render of this single outlet.** A cycle wishing to harden them should
  pair-render this URL — it is one cheap fetch and it has not been done.
- **A PRESS STATEMENT, NOT A PRIMARY ARTEFACT.** The base does not hold a KPMG-published page
  carrying this text. The wording is what the outlet reports KPMG said. The verbatim overlap with
  src-0036 on one sentence is good evidence that a real distributed statement exists, but neither
  outlet is KPMG.
- **A DATED SNAPSHOT OF A MOVING SITUATION.** "Reviewing the circumstances" and "while it
  investigates" describe an **unfinished** process as of 12 June 2026, ~7 weeks before this fetch.
  **Whether KPMG ever published a finding, named a cause, or restored or permanently withdrew the
  report is NOT established by this source and was NOT checked at cycle 99.** This is a concrete,
  cheap, untried follow-up and is flagged as such rather than answered.
- **THE REMOVAL IS NOT VERIFIED AT SOURCE.** This base has not checked KPMG's own websites for
  the report's presence or absence. Cycle 99 makes no absence claim about them.
- **NO CITATION COUNTS ARE TAKEN FROM THIS SOURCE.** The counts in this base's KPMG material come
  from src-0034 with the partition caveat recorded there.
- **DOES NOT BEAR ON open_questions[0].** A response is not a base rate. This source adds an
  incident and a response, and closes nothing about prevalence across CTI publishers.
- **KPMG IS A CONSULTANCY, NOT A CTI PUBLISHER PROPER.** As with EY Canada, the affected report is
  consulting thought-leadership that touches cyber/AI subject matter. The base has carried this
  scope caveat for the EY case since cycle 12 and it applies here identically; the topic is
  AI-generated CTI reliability, and a Big Four agentic-AI report is adjacent to, not squarely
  within, that population.

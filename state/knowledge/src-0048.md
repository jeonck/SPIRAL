# Summary

Collected by cycle 139 (T3) against `institutional-incident-real-world-impact`'s route-ledger
item (5)(iii) — **a non-Big-Four commercial response**, which the ledger has called the
highest-value missing data point on this issue since cycle 100 and which no cycle had collected.

Published **21 April 2026** by PYMNTS.com under its CPI banner (no author byline on the page).
It reports that **Sullivan & Cromwell LLP**, a US law firm, apologised to a US Bankruptcy Court
judge in Manhattan for a filing containing inaccurate citations that the reporting attributes to
AI. Cycle 139 fetched the URL at **two render families** — the direct form and
`r.jina.ai/https://www.pymnts.com/...` — and separately fetched a **second outlet carrying the
same story**, `https://molawyersmedia.com/2026/04/24/sullivan-cromwell-ai-errors-new-york-bankruptcy-case/`,
which the render identifies as a **Reuters wire piece of 24 April 2026 by Karen Freifeld and
Mike Scarcella**. The three renders agree on every element recorded below. **THE TWO OUTLETS ARE
NOT INDEPENDENT** — see Limitations.

**WHY THIS SOURCE MATTERS TO THIS BASE, STATED EXACTLY.** Cycle 100 (T2) wrote, on
`open_questions[5]`, that **"no cycle should read a finding into the 2/1 tally without a
NON-BIG-FOUR declining or volunteering case to break the confound"** — the confound being
whether the EY/KPMG (and later PwC) convergence on *reviewing the circumstances, name no cause*
reflects **commercial exposure** or **Big Four crisis-communications boilerplate**. This is the
first such case this base has ever held, and it is **also the first incident data point on this
issue whose discovery route does not pass through GPTZero**: the errors were caught by
**opposing counsel, Boies Schiller Flexner LLP**. The independence bar that five cycles have
recorded is, on this one narrow axis, met.

**AND THE SCOPE OBJECTION, STATED JUST AS LOUDLY, BECAUSE IT MAY DEFEAT THE WHOLE POINT.** The
artefact is a **court filing in a bankruptcy matter**, not a published report and not CTI. This
base already carries a standing caveat that EY, KPMG and PwC are consultancies and not CTI
publishers proper, and that their reports are adjacent to rather than squarely within the topic.
A law firm's emergency motion is **further out than that**, and it sits in a forum with a duty of
candour to a tribunal and an adversary with an incentive to check — an incentive environment
structurally unlike thought-leadership publishing. **CYCLE 139 DOES NOT DECIDE WHETHER THIS CASE
BELONGS IN THE RESPONSE POPULATION.** It enters the evidence, adds a candidate at status
`proposed` rather than `supported`, and opens **ctr-0069** so a T4 or T2 rules on admission
rather than a T3 admitting it silently.

# Key claims

1. **THE RESPONSE ATTRIBUTES A CONTROL FAILURE, WHERE EY, KPMG AND PwC ALL WITHHOLD. THIS IS THE
   DISCRIMINATING FACT AND IT MUST BE STATED WITH ITS BOUNDARY.** The reporting states that the
   firm maintains **"comprehensive policies and training requirements governing the use of AI
   tools in legal work"**; that **"the firm admitted those policies were not followed in this
   instance"**; and that an internal secondary review process **"did not identify the inaccurate
   citations generated by AI"**. **THAT IS A CONTROL-FAILURE ATTRIBUTION, NOT A ROOT-CAUSE
   NARRATIVE, AND THE DISTINCTION IS LOAD-BEARING.** Asked directly and without any answer
   supplied, **both renders of this URL returned ABSENT for a statement of what caused the
   inaccurate citations**, and both returned **ABSENT for any named AI product, tool, vendor or
   model**. So the accurate rendering is: the firm **attributes the citations to AI and names a
   failed internal check**, while stating neither who used what nor why. It is nevertheless
   **materially more than "we are reviewing the circumstances"**, which supplies nothing at all.
   A successor must not upgrade this to "named a cause" without qualification.
2. **THE APOLOGY IS A RESPONSE ELEMENT NO OTHER INSTITUTION IN THIS BASE EXHIBITS, AND IT IS
   VERBATIM.** Attributed to **Andrew Dietderich**, identified in the reporting as founder and
   co-head of the firm's restructuring group: **"I apologize on behalf of our entire team. I also
   called Boies Schiller Flexner LLP on Friday to thank them for bringing this matter to our
   attention and to apologize directly to them as well."** Neither ENISA, EY, KPMG nor PwC is
   recorded in this base as apologising to anyone. The sentence also **thanks the party that
   caught the defect**, which is the inverse of the posture the other four cases exhibit.
3. **THE REMEDY IS CORRECT-AND-RESUBMIT.** Both renders: **"A corrected version of the filing was
   later submitted."** On the withdraw-versus-retain axis that `open_questions[6]` records as the
   single surviving divergence dimension, this falls on the **correct** side with ENISA's
   retain-and-patch and PwC's correct-in-place, and away from EY's full withdrawal and KPMG's
   removal from **some** of its websites. **CAUTION**: a filing that is superseded on a docket is
   not the same object as a report left up on a website, and the axis may not transfer. Recorded
   as an observation, not as a fifth cell in the remedy table.
4. **THE DISCOVERY ROUTE IS NON-GPTZero AND THIS IS THE FIRST TIME ON THIS ISSUE.** All three
   renders name **Boies Schiller Flexner LLP** — opposing counsel — as the party that found the
   errors. No AI-detection vendor appears anywhere in the reporting. Every other institutional
   incident this base holds for this issue traces its discovery either to GPTZero (EY, KPMG, PwC)
   or to external researchers reported via Der Spiegel (ENISA, src-0004/src-0033).
5. **CASE, COURT, DATES AND WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED.** Matter: the **wind-down of Prince Global
   Holdings Limited**; court: the **US Bankruptcy Court in Manhattan**, before **Chief Judge
   Martin Glenn**. The apology letter is dated **18 April 2026**; this article is dated **21 April
   2026**; the Reuters wire render is dated **24 April 2026**. **THE DATE OF THE DEFECTIVE FILING
   IS ABSENT FROM EVERY RENDER CYCLE 139 RAN AND IS NOT ENTERED** — a search-result summary
   offered one, and cycle 139 deliberately did not take a date from a search snippet. **NOTE THE
   CHRONOLOGY**: April 2026 places this case **before** EY (May 2026), KPMG (June 2026) and PwC
   (July 2026), so it is the **earliest** of the commercial cases this base holds, not a late
   addition to a pattern.

# Limitations

- **THE TWO OUTLETS ARE NOT INDEPENDENT AND MUST NEVER BE COUNTED AS TWO.** The
  `molawyersmedia.com` render self-identifies as **Reuters** copy by named Reuters reporters, and
  the PYMNTS/CPI piece is a syndicated aggregation of the same story. Their near-identical
  wording is evidence that a single wire report exists, **not** corroboration by a second
  newsroom. This base has recorded four times that **outlet count is not independence**; the rule
  applies here with full force.
- **A PRESS ACCOUNT, NOT THE PRIMARY ARTEFACT.** This base does **not** hold the letter to the
  court, the defective motion, the corrected filing, or any docket entry. Everything above is
  what the reporting says the firm said. The primary route is a US bankruptcy docket, which this
  agent's tooling has never accessed and which is **not** recorded as attempted.
- **THE BOUNDARY BETWEEN QUOTATION AND PARAPHRASE IS NOT FULLY RESOLVABLE FROM THESE RENDERS.**
  "the firm admitted those policies were not followed in this instance" reads as reporter
  paraphrase with an embedded quotation; "did not identify the inaccurate citations generated by
  AI" is presented as a quoted fragment. Cycle 139 could not determine from either render whether
  these fragments are quoted from the letter itself or from a separate firm statement. **A
  SUCCESSOR MUST NOT PRESENT EITHER FRAGMENT AS A SENTENCE OF THE LETTER.**
- **RENDER COVERAGE DIFFERED BUT DID NOT CONFLICT.** The direct render was asked eight questions
  that did not include the policies-followed limb; the r.jina.ai render was asked for it and
  returned it. No render returned an answer contradicting another. This is a coverage difference
  arising from the questions asked, and it is **not** a two-render disagreement.
- **OUT-OF-DOMAIN, AND FURTHER OUT THAN ANY OTHER CASE ON THIS ISSUE.** A bankruptcy motion is
  not a threat report, not a consulting publication and not CTI. See ctr-0069: whether this case
  may enter the response population of `candidate_resolutions[3]` is a **scope ruling reserved to
  a T4 or T2**, and cycle 139 does not make it.
- **IT INTRODUCES A NEW CONFOUND WHILE BREAKING AN OLD ONE.** It does break the Big-Four/commercial
  confound cycle 100 named. But a filing before a judge carries a professional duty of candour and
  an adversary with a direct incentive to check the citations; a thought-leadership PDF carries
  neither. **FORUM** is therefore a live alternative explanation of the response difference, and no
  source in this base tests it.
- **NO COUNT AND NO BASE RATE.** The reporting gives no number of inaccurate citations, and this
  source bears on `open_questions[0]` (prevalence across CTI publishers) **not at all**.
- **NO CTI MULTI-SOURCE CREDIT.** Under this base's standing CTI exclusion rule, a law-firm
  bankruptcy filing may not be counted toward CTI multi-source support for any claim.

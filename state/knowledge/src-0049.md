# Summary

Collected by cycle 148 (T1) against the **one thing** `institutional-incident-real-world-impact`'s
`open_questions[5]` has asked for since its cycle-139 update: **a second non-Big-Four case whose
artefact is a PUBLISHED REPORT rather than a court filing**, which would settle both the scope
objection and the forum confound that `src-0048` opened. **THIS IS THAT CASE, AND IT IS A STRONGER
FIT THAN THE COMMISSION ASKED FOR**, because the responding institution is a **national government
department** and the artefact is a **policy document published in the Government Gazette for public
comment** — a voluntary publication with no tribunal, no duty of candour and no adversary.

**WHAT HAPPENED.** South Africa's **Minister of Communications and Digital Technologies, Solly
Malatsi**, announced on **26 April 2026** the **withdrawal of the Draft National Artificial
Intelligence Policy** after its reference list was found to contain fictitious sources. This page is
published by **SAnews.gov.za, the South African Government News Agency** (part of the Government
Communication and Information System) — i.e. **the government's own channel reporting its own
minister's statement**, which is the closest thing to a primary institutional response this base can
reach without the departmental PDF.

**RENDERS.** Cycle 148 fetched this URL at **two render families** — the direct form and
`r.jina.ai/https://www.sanews.gov.za/...`. They **agree on every sentence they share**; the
`r.jina.ai` render delivered **more** of the statement and self-reported its last body line as the
consequence-management sentence, so its ABSENTs (below) are attested over text actually delivered,
per [387]. Cycle 148 additionally fetched **three independent newsrooms** carrying the same
statement — `techcentral.co.za` (see `src-0050`), `dailymaverick.co.za` and `mg.co.za`.

**WHY THE CORROBORATION STRUCTURE MATTERS AND HOW IT DIFFERS FROM `src-0048`.** `src-0048` rests on
**one Reuters wire carried by two non-independent outlets**, and this base has recorded four times
that outlet count is not independence. Here the underlying artefact is **the minister's own public
statement**, reported by **the government's own agency plus three separately-owned South African
newsrooms with three different named bylines**. That is still **one primary utterance**, not four
independent findings — **NO CYCLE MAY COUNT THIS AS FOUR SOURCES** — but the utterance itself is
institutional and first-party rather than a reporter's reconstruction, which is what `src-0048`
could not supply.

# Key claims

1. **THE FIRST INSTITUTION IN THIS BASE TO NAME AI AS THE CAUSE IN ITS OWN VOICE, AND IT IS
   NON-BIG-FOUR AND ITS ARTEFACT IS A PUBLISHED DOCUMENT.** Verbatim, both renders:
   **"The most plausible explanation is that AI-generated citations were included without proper
   verification."** This is materially further than anything EY, KPMG or PwC produced (all three
   *review the circumstances, name no cause*) and further than `src-0048`'s **control-failure
   attribution**, which named a failed internal check but never what caused the citations.
   **BOUNDARY, AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING**: the minister's sentence is **HEDGED** — *most plausible
   explanation*, not *this is what happened* — and **no render of this page names any AI product,
   tool, model or vendor** (ABSENT, both renders, asked directly without an answer supplied). So the
   accurate rendering is **a hedged first-party causal attribution to AI-generated citations plus a
   verification failure**, and a successor must carry the hedge. See `src-0053` for a documented case
   of secondary coverage **stripping that hedge**.
2. **THE RESPONSE SHAPE IS WITHDRAW + CONCEDE INSTITUTIONAL FAULT + PROMISE CONSEQUENCES, AND NO
   OTHER CASE IN THIS BASE COMBINES THOSE.** Verbatim (`r.jina.ai` render, full statement; the
   direct render carries the first and third of these):
   - "Following revelations that the Draft National Artificial Intelligence Policy published for
     public comment contains various fictitious sources in its reference list, we initiated internal
     questions, which have now confirmed that this was the case."
   - "This failure is not a mere technical issue but has compromised the integrity and credibility of
     the draft policy. As such, I am withdrawing the Draft National Artificial Intelligence Policy."
   - "The Department of Communications and Digital Technologies did not deliver on the standard that
     is acceptable for an institution entrusted with the role to lead South Africa's digital policy
     environment."
   - "This should not have happened. In fact, this unacceptable lapse proves why vigilant human
     oversight over the use of artificial intelligence is critical."
   - "It's a lesson we take with humility. I want to reassure the country that we are treating this
     matter with the gravity it deserves. There will be consequence management for those responsible
     for drafting and quality assurance."
   On the **withdraw-versus-retain** axis of `open_questions[6]` this falls on the **withdraw** side
   with EY, against ENISA's retain-and-patch, PwC's correct-in-place and `src-0048`'s

3. **AN APOLOGY IS ABSENT, AND SO IS ANY DETECTION VENDOR.** The word *apologise/apologize* does

# Limitations

- **A GOVERNMENT AGENCY REPORTING ITS OWN MINISTER IS FIRST-PARTY, NOT INDEPENDENT.** SAnews is the
  state news agency. Its value here is *proximity to the institutional utterance*, which is exactly
  what this issue tracks; it is **not** corroboration of the underlying facts and must never be
  treated as an independent check on them.
- **ONE UTTERANCE, FOUR CARRIERS.** The government agency and the three newsrooms all report **one
  ministerial statement**. Per this base's standing rule that outlet count is not independence,
  **this is a single primary source of the response**, however many pages carry it.
- **A TRANSCRIPTION DIVERGENCE BETWEEN CARRIERS, RECORDED AND NOT RESOLVED.** SAnews renders the
  oversight sentence with **"proves why"**; `mg.co.za` renders it **"underscores why"** and drops
  *In fact*. `dailymaverick.co.za` carries an additional sentence — **"South Africans deserve
  better."** — that is **absent from the SAnews article** (attested over the region the `r.jina.ai`
  render delivered, which self-reports ending at the consequence-management sentence). Cycle 148
  cannot determine whether the outlets quote a longer statement, a media briefing or a parliamentary
  remark. **THE SAnews WORDING IS TREATED AS AUTHORITATIVE HERE BECAUSE IT IS THE GOVERNMENT'S OWN
  CHANNEL; the divergence is recorded as evidence that quoted institutional sentences drift between
  carriers, not as a contradiction.**
- **NO PRIMARY DEPARTMENTAL ARTEFACT.** This base does **not** hold the DCDT media statement as
  issued, the Government Gazette notice, the withdrawn policy PDF, or any parliamentary transcript.
  Whether the withdrawn document is still retrievable anywhere is **untested**.
- **NO COUNTS OF ANY KIND.** See key claim 4. This source bears on `open_questions[0]` (a base rate
  across CTI publishers) **not at all**.
- **OUT OF DOMAIN, THOUGH LESS SO THAN `src-0048`.** A national AI policy is not a threat report and
  not CTI. It is, however, a **technology-governance document from a digital-technologies ministry**,
  which sits closer to this base's topic than a bankruptcy motion and arguably no further out than
  ENISA, an EU agency, already admitted to the population.
- **WHETHER THIS CASE MAY ENTER THE RESPONSE POPULATION OF `candidate_resolutions[3]` IS A SCOPE
  RULING AND CYCLE 148 DOES NOT MAKE IT.** A T1 adds evidence; it does not restructure the issue
  graph or move a score. Note for whoever rules: the **forum** confound `ctr-0069` opened against
  `src-0048` **does not apply to this case at all**, which is precisely why it was collected.

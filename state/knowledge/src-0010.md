## Summary

The SECOND of the two ENISA reports named in the src-0004 incident. src-0004 (Heise, via
Der Spiegel) says "two ENISA reports, published last October and November" were affected.
Cycle 12 located both publication pages; this is the November one. Together with src-0009
it converts Heise's "two reports, October and November" from secondary reporting into a
claim checkable against ENISA's own artefacts — and both check out on dates and on the
link-related nature of the defect.

## Key claims

1. The page carries a verbatim revision notice: "Revision Notice – Version 1.2 (09 January
   2026): This publication has been updated to correct some broken links and typos." The
   file is `ENISA Public Administration TL 2024 - v1.2.pdf`, served from a `2026-01/` path,
   against a stated publication date of November 6, 2025.
2. Both affected ENISA reports were therefore revised on the SAME date, 09 January 2026,
   and both revision notices describe the fix in mechanical terms (links; links and typos)
   with no mention of cause.
3. This page likewise contains no mention of artificial intelligence, AI or generative AI
   being used to produce the report (verified absent 2026-07-29).

## Limitations of this source

- Same limitation as src-0009: the absence of an AI disclosure was established from the
  rendered landing page, not from the PDF's front matter.
- Note a naming oddity that a future cycle should not trip over: the report is dated
  November 2025 and is the "Public Administration" sectorial threat landscape, but the
  filename says "TL 2024". This is ENISA's own filename; it is recorded here as observed,
  not corrected, and it is not evidence of anything.
- Like src-0009, it gives no footnote-error count and assigns no cause.

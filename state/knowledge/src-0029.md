## Summary

A survey of classifier calibration covering proper scoring rules and other evaluation
metrics, visualisation (reliability diagrams), and post-hoc calibration methods. The
parts relevant to this base are Section 3 (from proper scoring rules to calibration),
Section 3.1 (decompositions of proper scoring rules) and Section 4 (evaluation metrics
and visualisation).

## Key claims (verbatim-supported)

1. **The Brier score is a strictly proper scoring rule, and a proper scoring rule
   decomposes into calibration loss and refinement loss.** Section 3, verbatim: "Both
   Brier score and log-loss are strictly proper scoring rules." Section 3.1, verbatim:
   "The decomposition of proper scoring rule φ into calibration loss and refinement loss
   is then the sum of expected loss between S and C and expected loss between C and Y".
   This **independently corroborates src-0028 key_claim 1** from a different team and a
   different venue.

2. **The terminology of the decomposition is old and has drifted, which is why the base
   kept conflating the two quantities.** Section 3.1, verbatim: "Validity, later known as
   reliability (Murphy & Winkler, 1977), is now often called calibration loss (Kull &
   Flach, 2015)...Sharpness, which is now commonly called refinement loss and was also
   known as resolution (Murphy & Winkler, 1977)".

3. **Binned and reliability-diagram-based calibration assessment has documented
   artefacts, and the survey's own guidance is to use calibration metrics TOGETHER WITH
   proper scoring rules rather than instead of them.** Section 4.1, verbatim: "Even
   perfectly calibrated scores may result in a visually non-calibrated reliability
   diagram with a deviation of up to half the bin width from the diagonal". Section 4.4,
   verbatim: "Binary-MCE can be very sensitive to bins with few instances". Section 4.4,
   guidance verbatim: "It is good practice to use them together with the proper scoring
   rules presented in Section 3, to get a full evaluation".

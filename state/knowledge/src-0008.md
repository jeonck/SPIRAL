## Summary

arXiv 2605.06910v1 (submitted 7 May 2026, cs.CR, CC BY 4.0, 11 pages / 2 figures /
8 tables). Authors: Jaime Morales, Sergio Pastrana, Juan Tapiador (Universidad Carlos
III de Madrid, Spain) — a third team, independent of both src-0003's and src-0007's
authors.

Added opportunistically in cycle 9's T3 alongside src-0007. Scope note, read this
before citing it: this paper measures IoC recovery from **JavaScript source code under
deliberate adversarial concealment**, NOT IoC extraction from narrative threat reports.
It is therefore NOT a replication of src-0003 and must not be used as one. Its value to
this topic is as a boundary condition on the "mechanical extraction is the reliable
part of CTI" hypothesis, and as the knowledge base's first evidence on adversary-side
evasion of LLM extraction — a threat model no other collected source covers.

## Key claims

1. Recognising an IoC that is present in plain text is essentially free for current
   LLMs: at baseline (P0) and through four levels of syntactic obfuscation (P1-P4,
   e.g. variable renaming, Base64 encoding), detection is ~100% for ChatGPT (GPT-4
   Turbo), Claude 3 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro and Grok-2.
2. Cryptographic concealment collapses this to near zero: at P5-P6 (XOR and AES-256)
   detection falls to roughly 0-1%, and the failure persists through the combined
   transformations P7-P12 (~95%+ misses). The authors frame encryption as the critical
   gap in LLM-based code analysis.
3. Hallucination rates (reporting an IoC that is not there) were low but nonzero and
   varied by vendor: Anthropic 0.11%, ChatGPT 0.23%, Gemini 4.8%, Grok and Cohere 0%.
4. The paper does not cite or compare against LANCE or PRISM (arXiv 2506.11325).

## Relevant quotes

Abstract (verbatim):

> "Software obfuscation and encryption present persistent challenges for program
> comprehension and security analysis, particularly when adversaries conceal Indicators
> of Compromise (IoCs) such as IP addresses within source code. While Large Language
> Models (LLMs) have recently demonstrated remarkable progress in code reasoning and
> transformation, their resilience against adversarial concealment techniques remains
> largely uncharted. This paper introduces a systematic benchmark for secret detection
> under adversarial code transformations, designed to evaluate the capacity of LLMs to
> recover IoCs embedded in obfuscated and encrypted JavaScript programs. We construct a
> dataset of 336 programs, progressively transformed through 12 levels of obfuscation
> and cryptographic concealment (including XOR and AES-256), to emulate realistic threat
> scenarios. An automated evaluation framework standardizes LLM queries and responses,
> enabling reproducible, large-scale testing across diverse models. Our results reveal a
> dichotomy: while LLMs exhibit high success against lightweight transformations such as
> variable renaming and Base64 encoding, encryption-based concealment severely degrades
> detection performance."

## Limitations of this source

- **Different task from the rest of this issue.** Input is source code, not a threat
  report. The metric is detection rate for a known planted indicator, not precision or
  recall over an open set of candidate indicators in prose. The near-100% plain-text
  figure therefore does NOT corroborate src-0003's 97.6% F1: finding one planted IP in
  a program is not the same problem as separating malicious indicators from benign
  artifacts in a report, which is the specific difficulty src-0003 exists to address.
- Single indicator family in the concealment experiments (IP addresses); synthetic
  transformation pipeline rather than captured real-world malware.
- 336 programs is small, and the paper's own framing is a first systematic look at an
  "largely uncharted" question.
- Retrieved via automated fetch of the arXiv /html and /abs pages; the abstract was
  captured verbatim, but the per-level numbers above came from the fetcher's reading of
  the results tables and were not re-checked row-by-row against the PDF. Treat the
  ~100% / ~0-1% / hallucination-rate figures as approximate pending a verbatim table
  pull, and re-verify before they are used to support a scored resolution.

## Summary

Authors Yuqiao Meng, Luoxi Tang, Feiyang Yu, Jinyuan Jia, Guanhua Yan, Ping Yang, and
Zhaohan Xi (arXiv 2509.23573, cs.CR/cs.AI; submitted 2025-09-28, revised through v5
2026-05-28) argue that LLM unreliability in CTI is not primarily a generic
model-architecture problem (e.g. "hallucination" in the abstract sense) but is driven
by properties of the threat landscape itself: CTI evidence is heterogeneous, volatile,
crowdsourced, and fragmented. They build a human-in-the-loop categorization framework
(explicitly designed to avoid the brittleness of automated "LLM-as-a-judge" labeling)
to classify LLM failure modes across the CTI lifecycle without relying on LLM
self-judgment, identify three domain-specific cognitive failure patterns, validate them
via causal interventions, and test targeted mitigations. This is the first source in
our knowledge base that measures reliability across multiple distinct CTI sub-tasks
within one consistent study design/methodology (as opposed to synthesizing across
separate single-task benchmarks run by different teams with different models), making
it directly relevant to whether "task-dependent reliability" is itself an empirically
supported property rather than an artifact of comparing incommensurable benchmarks.

## Key claims

1. The paper organizes CTI work into four pipeline stages -- Contextualization
   (enriching raw observations with identifiers/timelines), Attribution (linking
   threats to adversaries), Prediction (forecasting exploitation likelihood/impact),
   and Mitigation (recommending patches/detection/response) -- and finds that its three
   identified failure patterns (spurious correlations from superficial metadata,
   contradictory knowledge from conflicting sources, and constrained generalization to
   emerging threats) occur across ALL FOUR stages, not concentrated in
   reasoning-/generation-heavy stages. This is evidence against a simple binary
   "mechanical extraction is reliable / open-ended reasoning is not" framing: reliability
   varies task-by-task even within what would loosely be called the "extraction"/
   "contextualization" end of the pipeline.
2. Within-study reliability varies enormously by specific sub-task under otherwise
   consistent methodology (Table 2): e.g. "Source Reliability Scoring" reaches AUC 0.912
   for GPT-5 but only 0.547 for a specialized model on the same task, while
   "Infrastructure Reuse" peaks at F1 0.754 for a specialized agent vs. 0.688 for a
   general model -- overall F1 range across evaluated tasks/models is roughly 0.20-0.90.
   This is a direct, single-study demonstration that reliability is task-dependent,
   complementing (not merely restating) the cross-study synthesis already in this issue
   graph (src-0001/0002/0003/0005, each a separate benchmark/team/model-set).
3. Targeted mitigations (retrieval-augmented and inference-level causal-intervention
   strategies, validated via a "counterfactual consistency check") produced measurable
   but task-size-dependent gains: e.g. "Threat Actor Linking" (an attribution-adjacent
   task) improved by +0.055 accuracy for a Claude model after intervention, "Event
   Timeline Construction" improved by an average of ~+0.08 across models, and
   "False Flag Detection" improved by up to +0.115 F1 (Mixtral). These gains are
   substantially smaller in absolute/relative terms than LANCE's jump from ~72-86%
   (legacy tools) to 97.6% F1 on IoC extraction (src-0003), suggesting that even
   effective mitigation strategies close the reliability gap less completely on
   attribution/reasoning-adjacent tasks than a narrow, human-validated pipeline does
   on mechanical indicator extraction -- though the two are not a controlled
   apples-to-apples comparison (different tasks, metrics, and mitigation types).

## Relevant quotes

## Limitations of this source

- Retrieved via automated HTML-to-summary tooling (fast-model read of the arXiv HTML
  render), not a manual read of the full PDF with tables reproduced by hand; treat
  exact numeric figures (Table 2/3/6/7 values) as high-confidence but not
  independently hand-verified, consistent with the same caveat already applied to
  src-0001/src-0002/src-0003 in this knowledge base.
- A first WebFetch attempt against the raw PDF URL for this same paper produced a
  response describing a taxonomy of "lower-risk mechanical tasks" vs. "higher-risk
  cognitive tasks" that does NOT match the abstract or the HTML-render summary
  obtained subsequently -- that PDF-based response appears to have been fabricated/
  hallucinated by the fetch tool's summarizer rather than grounded in the actual
  (binary, non-parseable) PDF content, and was discarded. Only the abstract-page and
  HTML-render responses (which are mutually consistent) are reflected in this file.
  This is a caveat about this cycle's own research process, not about the paper.
  itself, but a future cycle re-using this paper should prefer the HTML render
  (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23573) or a manual PDF read over re-fetching the raw
  PDF with automated tooling.
- Does not test IoC extraction, ATT&CK-technique mapping, or threat-actor attribution
  in the specific benchmark forms used by src-0002/src-0003 (CTI-ATE, CTI-TAA, PRISM),
  so it corroborates the general principle of task-dependent reliability without
  being directly comparable, task-for-task, to those benchmarks' specific numbers.

  during review; this summary reflects the content as fetched 2026-07-28 and has not

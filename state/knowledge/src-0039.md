# Method — why this counts as independent

> "we query a locally hosted Elasticsearch index constructed from Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex.
> Each parsed title is used to retrieve candidate matches, and we determine whether any candidate
> constitutes a valid match using string similarity-based criteria. This approach matches 95.1% of
> references."

> "we route all unmatched entries through an LLM-based cleaning step. Using GPT-4o-mini, we
> identify and exclude reference strings that are unlikely to be academic references"

> "By manually validating many of the unmatched cases, we find that false negatives most often
> arise"

The verification instrument is **database resolution against Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex**, not
a hallucination-detection product. That is a genuinely different route from GPTZero's Hallucination
Check tool, which is what makes the rate comparison below meaningful rather than circular.

# Key claim for `incident-evidence-vendor-concentration`

This source demonstrates, by existing, **what a prevalence claim about fabricated citations
actually requires**: a stated corpus, a stated denominator (111M references / 2.5M papers), a
stated verification method, and per-corpus rates with a stated as-of date. The GPTZero
"Chasing the Hallucinations" consulting series publishes **none of these four things**. The
contrast is now concrete rather than rhetorical.

# Convergence worth recording

Three mutually independent routes now put the **per-paper** academic rate at roughly **1%**:

- `src-0011` (GhostCite): 2.2M citations across 56,381 papers, **1.01%** of papers at Security
  venues contain at least one invalid citation.
- `src-0038` (Ansari, reporting GPTZero's NeurIPS sweep): 53 of 4,841 scanned, **~1%** of accepted
  papers.
- This source: **0.39%** of arXiv *references* — a per-reference rate, so **not** directly
  comparable with the two per-paper figures above, but the same order of magnitude once a paper
  carries tens of references.

**THE UNIT MISMATCH IS REAL AND IS NOT BEING PAPERED OVER**: per-paper "at least one" rates and
per-reference rates are different quantities and this base has been bitten by exactly that
confusion before (`ctr-0049`, episode-level at-least-one vs per-decision). The convergence is
offered as an order-of-magnitude observation, not an arithmetic identity.

# Other findings, recorded but not load-bearing here

The abstract reports a rise in fabricated citations following LLM adoption, concentration in
fields with high AI adoption and in papers with linguistic markers of AI-assisted composition, and
that errors "disproportionately credit already-established and male researchers". The last is a
bias finding outside this topic's scope; it is noted so that a later cycle does not treat it as
newly discovered.

# Limitations

- Preprint, not peer-reviewed at time of access.
- The 146,932 figure is self-described as a "conservative estimate"; it is an estimate, not a count.
- Rates are "as of August 2025" and this base accessed the paper in August 2026 — the figures are
  a year stale relative to access.

## Summary

arXiv 2601.21083 [cs.AI], submitted 28 Jan 2026, last revised 6 Feb 2026 (v3). Sole
author: Jarrod Barnes. Code at https://github.com/jbarnes850/opensec-env.

Why it was added (cycle 15, T1 targeting `consistency-calibration-as-failure-mode`):
of the four sources collected this cycle it is the one operating closest to a real CTI
/ SOC workflow — incident response agents deciding whether to contain a threat — and it
is the only one testing the **current** model generation (GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5,
Gemini 3, DeepSeek 3.2), which no source in this base previously did. It measures
calibration behaviourally: not "is the stated confidence number right" but "does the
agent act before it has gathered evidence".

Its single most transferable finding is a clean separation that the rest of this
knowledge base has never been able to make. Every other source measures accuracy and
infers overconfidence from the gap between accuracy and stated certainty. OpenSec finds
that when these agents act they are *right about the threat* — the failure is entirely
in acting without having checked. In its own words, "the calibration gap is not in
detection but in restraint."

Read this source as an operational analogue of src-0007's triage asymmetry (recall
0.90–1.00, precision 0.27–0.40: the models accept nearly everything). Two independent
studies, different teams, different model generations, same shape of failure: the
models under-refuse.

## Key claims

1. **Frontier IR agents systematically over-trigger, and the effect is large.** Over 40
   standard-tier episodes per model: GPT-5.2 executes containment in 100% of episodes
   with an 82.5% false-positive rate, acting at step 4.1; DeepSeek 3.2 contains in 92.5%
   of episodes at 65% FP; Gemini 3 in 75% at 57.5% FP; Claude Sonnet 4.5 in 62.5% at 45%
   FP with time-to-first-containment 10.6. The author classifies GPT-5.2 as "the only
   model classified as uncalibrated" and Sonnet 4.5 as showing "partial calibration",
   concluding that "calibration is not reliably present across frontier models."
2. **The failure is restraint, not detection — and this is a mechanism claim the rest of
   the base could not make.** "All models correctly identify the ground-truth threat when
   they act; the calibration gap is not in detection but in restraint." Accuracy-based
   evaluation would score these agents well; the reliability defect is invisible to it.
3. **Evidence-gating is measurably rare across all four models.** EGAR — "the fraction of
   containment actions preceded by fetching trusted evidence about the target entity;
   it measures whether the model checks before it acts" — is 0.375 (GPT-5.2), 0.392
   (Sonnet 4.5), 0.429 (Gemini 3), 0.542 (DeepSeek 3.2). Every model acts without first
   checking trusted evidence on a majority or near-majority of its containment actions.

## Relevant quotes

Abstract, verbatim, from https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21083:

> "As large language models (LLMs) improve, so do their offensive applications: frontier
> agents now generate working exploits for under $50 in compute (Heelan, 2026).
> Defensive incident response (IR) agents must keep pace, but existing benchmarks
> conflate action execution with correct execution, hiding calibration failures when
> agents process adversarial evidence. We introduce OpenSec, a dual-control reinforcement
> learning (RL) environment that evaluates IR agents under realistic prompt injection
> scenarios with execution-based scoring: time-to-first-containment (TTFC),
> evidence-gated action rate (EGAR), blast radius, and per-tier injection violation
> rates. Evaluating four frontier models on 40 standard-tier episodes each, we find
> consistent over-triggering: GPT-5.2 executes containment in 100% of episodes with 82.5%
> false positive rate, acting at step 4 before gathering sufficient evidence. Claude
> Sonnet 4.5 shows partial calibration (62.5% containment, 45% FP, TTFC of 10.6),
> suggesting calibration is not reliably present across frontier models. All models
> correctly identify the ground-truth threat when they act; the calibration gap is not in
> detection but in restraint. Code available at
> https://github.com/jbarnes850/opensec-env."

## Limitations of this source

- **THIS IS THE WEAKEST-PROVENANCE SOURCE ADDED THIS CYCLE AND SHOULD CARRY THE LEAST
  WEIGHT.** Single-author preprint, filed under cs.AI rather than cs.CR, no stated venue
  or peer review, and no author affiliation given on the listing page. It should not be
  used as the sole support for any candidate_resolution.
- **SMALL n.** 40 episodes per model, from an evaluation pool of 60 seeds of which 20
  are standard-tier. Differences between adjacent models (e.g. Gemini 3's 75% vs
  DeepSeek 3.2's 92.5% containment) rest on a handful of episodes and no confidence
  intervals or significance tests are reported in the material fetched. Treat the
  ordering of the middle two models as unresolved; only the GPT-5.2-vs-Sonnet-4.5
  contrast is large enough to be robust to that concern.
- **"CALIBRATION" HERE IS NOT ECE/BRIER CALIBRATION.** The paper does not measure whether
  a stated confidence matches empirical correctness. It measures action thresholds —
  false-positive rate and evidence-gating before acting. This is legitimately the
  abstention / selective-prediction axis named by the target issue, but it is a
  DIFFERENT construct from src-0001's and src-0013's ECE, and the two must not be pooled
  or averaged. A model can be perfectly ECE-calibrated and still over-trigger, and vice
  versa.
- **SCOPE: incident response, not CTI production.** Containment decisions in a simulated
  RL environment under prompt injection are adjacent to, not identical with, the CTI
  tasks this project scopes (IOC extraction, TTP mapping, threat reports). It is closer
  to CTI operations than src-0013 or src-0014, but it is still not a measurement on
  threat-report material.
- **Synthetic environment.** Episodes are generated seeds in a dual-control RL
  environment, not real incidents from a production SOC. Contrast src-0007, whose
  material comes from a real company's daily CTI workflow. Ecological validity is
  asserted ("realistic prompt injection scenarios") but not established against real
  incident data.
- Retrieved by automated fetch of `/abs` (abstract, authorship, dates) and `/html/v3`
  (Table 1, EGAR definition). The abstract is verbatim-confirmed and Table 1 was
  returned whole. The abstract says GPT-5.2 acts "at step 4" while the body says
  "step 4.1" and Table 1 gives TTFC 4.1 — a rounding difference in the abstract, not a
  discrepancy.

# Summary

TechCrunch's report on the KPMG incident, by **Anthony Ha**, **13 June 2026** — one day after
src-0034 (the GPTZero investigation) and src-0035 (The Register). Collected for one specific
reason: **this base has been repeatedly burned by single-outlet reporting on this issue**, and
`open_questions[2]` records at length how three "corroborating" outlets for the ENISA figure all
turned out to be recounting Der Spiegel. This source is the independence check on the KPMG
response, and it is a **partial pass**.

**WHAT IT CONFIRMS INDEPENDENTLY:** the fourth sentence of KPMG's statement is returned
**verbatim and identically** to src-0035's rendering of it. Two outlets reproducing a sentence
character-for-character is good evidence that a distributed press statement with that wording
exists.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT MAKE INDEPENDENT — AND THIS IS THE SOURCE'S MOST IMPORTANT CONTRIBUTION:**
TechCrunch attributes the substantive corroboration to the **Financial Times**, not to its own
reporting. It is the FT to which GPTZero spoke, and the FT to which the four named organisations
denied the report's claims about them. **So the KPMG story runs through a two-outlet upstream
chain (GPTZero and the FT), and The Register and TechCrunch are downstream of it — exactly the
structure that made the ENISA corroboration non-independent.** A cycle counting "how many outlets
confirm KPMG" would be making the cycle-12 error again.

**IT ALSO ADDS A GENUINELY NEW EVIDENCE TYPE THIS BASE HAS NEVER HELD: named third parties
denying, on the record, claims a report made about them.** Every prior incident in this issue was
established by *dead links* — citations that failed to resolve. Here the failure mode is a
*fabricated factual assertion about a real, identifiable organisation*, and the organisation
itself refuted it. That is a materially different and harder-to-detect defect than a broken URL,
and no link-resolution sweep of the kind `open_questions[0]` proposes would have caught it.

# Key claims

1. **ONE SENTENCE OF KPMG'S STATEMENT IS CONFIRMED VERBATIM ACROSS TWO INDEPENDENT OUTLETS:
   "We expect all our people to follow our guidelines on the responsible use of AI, including
   human oversight to validate content and verify independent sources."** Returned
   character-identical by TechCrunch (this source) and The Register (src-0035). TechCrunch also
   independently reports the remedy — that a KPMG spokesperson said the firm removed the report
   from its websites while conducting its own investigation. **The other three sentences of the
   statement quoted in src-0035 are NOT reproduced here and remain single-sourced.**
2. **THE KPMG CORROBORATION CHAIN IS NOT AS INDEPENDENT AS THE OUTLET COUNT SUGGESTS: TechCrunch
   attributes the substantive findings to the Financial Times.** Verbatim: "GPTZero told the
   Financial Times that the inaccuracies stemmed from AI hallucinations", and "UBS, the U.K.'s
   National Health Service, Swiss Federal Railways, and Transport for London all informed the FT
   that the report's claims about their AI usage were either untrue or misleading." The upstream
   is GPTZero + the FT; downstream outlets recount them. **No cycle may treat The Register and
   TechCrunch as two independent confirmations of the FT-sourced material.** The base does not
   hold the FT article itself, and obtaining it is an untried route.
3. **A NEW FAILURE MODE FOR THIS ISSUE: FOUR NAMED ORGANISATIONS PUBLICLY DENIED CLAIMS THE
   REPORT MADE ABOUT THEM — UBS, the UK's National Health Service, Swiss Federal Railways, and
   Transport for London — telling the FT the claims about their AI usage were "either untrue or
   misleading".** Every previously documented incident in this issue (ENISA, EY Canada, curl) was
   surfaced by **unresolvable citations**. This one was additionally surfaced by **fabricated
   assertions about identifiable third parties who could and did rebut them**. Detection-wise
   these are not the same problem: a dead link is mechanically checkable, whereas a plausible
   false claim about a named organisation is not detectable by link resolution at all. This bears
   directly on `open_questions[4]` (would any pre-release check have caught it?) — **a citation
   verifier would not have.**

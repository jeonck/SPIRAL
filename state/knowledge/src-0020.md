# Summary

Ryan et al. (arXiv 2606.18166, v1 submitted 16 Jun 2026) evaluate **seven open-source LLMs
from 8B to 236B parameters** on multi-label MITRE ATT&CK technique classification over
sentences drawn from 83 complex unstructured CTI reports (The DFIR Report). The headline
result is very low: **the best micro-F1 across all configurations is 0.22**.

Two things make this source valuable to `ttp-attack-mapping-reliability` beyond the number
itself:

1. It is a **third independent team** on this task — no author or institution overlap with
   CTIBench (src-0002, RIT/Rastogi) or CyberThreat-Eval (src-0007/src-0017, xschen-beb).
2. It supplies the **first inter-annotator agreement statistic in this base** for ATT&CK
   technique labelling: mean **κ = 0.68**, with only 57.0% of techniques clearing κ ≥ 0.7.
   This bears directly on open_questions[1], which has gone twenty-two cycles unfilled.

It also states its label granularity explicitly — **parent techniques only, sub-techniques
omitted** — which is the property that governs commensurability with src-0007's exact-ID
scorer.

# Key claims

1. **BEST MICRO-F1 IS 0.22, ON PARENT-LEVEL ATT&CK TECHNIQUES, BY AN OPEN-SOURCE 236B MODEL.**
   Verbatim: *"Across all configurations, the maximum micro F1 score is 0.22, produced by DS236
   at (T=0.0, Shot=3, CoT =Yes)."* and, from the abstract, *"The highest-performing LLM
   achieved a micro-averaged F1 score of 0.22"*. The seven models, from Table I, are
   **DeepSeek-V2.5, GPT-OSS (120B), Llama 3.1 Instruct (70B), Gemma 3 (27B), GPT-OSS (20B),
   Gemma 3 (12B), Llama 3.1 Instruct (8B)**. The label space is parent-level only:
   *"To define a clear boundary for the classification task, we labeled each sentence against
   the 211 techniques from MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise v17.1 (August 2025), omitting the more
   granular sub-techniques."* Dataset: *"The final dataset comprises 2,076 sentences mapped to
   114 unique ATT&CK techniques, totaling 2,028 technique occurrences across 211 potential
   candidates"*, drawn from 83 complex unstructured CTI reports.

2. **FIRST INTER-ANNOTATOR AGREEMENT NUMBER IN THIS BASE FOR ATT&CK TECHNIQUE LABELLING —
   HUMAN EXPERTS AGREE ONLY SUBSTANTIALLY, NOT NEARLY.** Verbatim: *"Following the six-phase
   process, we reached a mean inter-annotator agreement of κ=0.68 across 114 techniques
   (Landis and Koch: substantial agreement [25]), with 65 techniques (57.0%) exceeding the
   κ≥0.7 quality threshold."* Cohen's κ was *"computed ... per technique to account for chance
   agreement"*. **This is an AGREEMENT statistic, not an accuracy baseline.** It does not say
   how well a human scores against a gold standard; it says two trained humans assigning
   ATT&CK techniques to the same sentence disagree often enough that 43% of techniques fall
   below a κ ≥ 0.7 quality bar. It is nonetheless the first quantitative evidence in this base
   for the long-asserted claim that ATT&CK technique assignment is a contested annotation
   task, and it was collected on **parent-level techniques**, i.e. on the *easier* labelling
   problem than sub-technique assignment.

3. **THE PAPER STATES ITS METRIC BUT NOT ITS ITEM-LEVEL CORRECTNESS RULE.** It says it reports
   micro-averaged precision, recall and F1, and glosses *"Micro average indicates how well a
   classifier performs on all the technique instances."* A verification-mode fetch on
   2026-07-31 explicitly targeting "exact match", "technique ID", "sub-technique", "parent
   technique", "we count" and "true positive" **found no sentence stating how a predicted
   technique is judged correct against ground truth**. So this source shares src-0002's defect
   in kind, though far less consequentially: because the label space is declared to be the 211
   parent techniques with sub-techniques omitted, the plausible matching rules collapse toward
   exact parent-ID set membership. **That is an inference and must be labelled as such.** Per
   rule (v) this ABSENT rests on a single fetch of a single URL form and is NOT confirmed
   absent; a successor citing the absence should re-check it.

# Limitations

- **UNREVIEWED PREPRINT AT v1.** `arxiv.org/abs/2606.18166` carries **no `Comments:` line and
  no `Journal reference:` line** (both checked ABSENT on 2026-07-31). There is no venue and no
  peer review. Version staleness is nil today (v1 is the only version) but must be re-checked.
- **THE MODELS ARE OPEN-SOURCE ONLY. THIS IS NOT A FRONTIER-MODEL MEASUREMENT AND DOES NOT
  ANSWER open_questions[0].** No GPT-5-class, Claude-4.5/Opus-class or Gemini-2.5-class model
  is evaluated. The 0.22 must NOT be read as evidence about 2026-era frontier capability.
- **SENTENCE-LEVEL, NOT REPORT-LEVEL, AND PARENT-LEVEL, NOT SUB-TECHNIQUE.** Both properties
  differ from src-0007's document-level free-form exact-sub-technique-ID setting and from
  src-0002's CTI-ATE. **The 0.22 is NOT directly comparable with src-0002's 0.6388 or with
  src-0007's 0.2787/0.2270** — the task format, the granularity, the corpus and the metric
  aggregation all differ. Any cycle placing these numbers on one axis must control for all
  four, and this base's ctr-0013 is a standing warning about exactly that move.
- Single corpus (The DFIR Report), acknowledged by the authors as a generalisation limit.
- The κ = 0.68 is reported as a **mean over 114 per-technique Cohen's κ values**; no
  confidence interval, and no per-technique distribution beyond the 57.0% figure, was captured.

### What the state stored

`key_claims[1]` stores the computation clause with an ellipsis, ending at "chance agreement":

> Cohen's kappa was 'computed ... per technique to account for chance agreement'.

and characterises the statistic as "a MEAN over 114 per-technique kappa values with no
confidence interval". Both statements are true as far as they go. Neither records **which
items the values were computed over**, and ctr-0015 relied on that silence when it called
this estimand "the only one of the three measured over an unrestricted item set".

### What the source says — the six-phase paragraph, verbatim and whole

Pulled from `https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18166v1` on 2026-08-01, then re-confirmed by a
**second, separate exact-string ask** on the literal strings `on a random 10% sample (195
sentences)` and `which was collected from both annotators` — both PRESENT, same clause.
Rule (xiii) was complied with deliberately: a summarised read can manufacture a defect that
is not there, and a false contradiction is more expensive than a missed one.

> We carried out the annotation in six phases: (i) Independent Labeling, where the
> annotators labeled the allocated reports independently; (ii) Initial Cross-Labeling, where
> we computed Cohen's Kappa (κ) per technique to account for chance agreement **on a random
> 10% sample (195 sentences), which was collected from both annotators**; (iii) Relabeling,
> where the annotators re-evaluated **981 low-agreement sentences (κ<0.7)**, split 460/521
> between first and second annotators; (iv) Secondary Cross-Labeling, where we checked 10%
> of the data relabeled in step-iii (98 sentences) to verify κ improvement; (v) Dispute
> Resolution, where we resolved conflicts via reconciliation meeting; and (vi) Dataset
> Aggregation, where we merged data of from all phases into the final ground-truth dataset.

(Bold added here for the two clauses the state did not hold; the source has no emphasis.)

### Three consequences

**(1) Precision.** The 114 per-technique kappa values are each estimated from a 195-sentence
sample, not from the 2,076-sentence corpus. The paper reports 2,028 technique occurrences
over 2,076 sentences, so a 10% sample carries on the order of 200 technique occurrences
spread across 114 techniques — **of the order of two positive instances per technique** — and
the mean is reported **with no confidence interval**. *The per-technique occurrence figure in
this paragraph is a cycle-47 derivation from two printed numbers and is labelled as such
under methodological rule (xii). The printed numbers are exact.*

**(2) A tension inside the source, not a state defect.** The headline sentence —

> Following the six-phase process, we reached a mean inter-annotator agreement of κ=0.68
> across 114 techniques (Landis and Koch: substantial agreement), with 65 techniques (57.0%)
> exceeding the κ≥0.7 quality threshold.

— re-verified PRESENT as one whole sentence, so the base's stored quotation of it is
**accurate**. But the paragraph computes kappa at phase (ii) of six, and phases (iii)
relabelling, (iv) a re-check "to verify κ improvement" and (v) dispute resolution all come
after it. "Following the six-phase process, we reached" and "computed at phase (ii) of six"
cannot both be read strictly. No sentence read so far states that the 0.68 was recomputed
after phase (iv). **Nobody may resolve this tension by picking the reading that suits them.**
Settling it is ctr-0016 step (iv), and it is open.

**(3) A number this base had never recorded — and it is better evidence than the 0.68.**
**981 of 2,076 sentences (47.3%, a cycle-47 derivation from two printed numbers) were
"low-agreement (κ<0.7)" and required relabelling.** That figure is measured over the *whole*
corpus rather than a sample, and it is the cleanest quantitative evidence in this base that
parent-level ATT&CK sentence labelling is a contested annotation task.

### What is withdrawn

ctr-0015's description of this source's kappa as "the only one of the three measured over an
unrestricted item set" is **false and is withdrawn**. All three pooled kappa values are now
known to be restricted or aggregated in some way:

| estimand | value | what it is computed over |
|---|---|---|
| src-0020 | 0.68 | mean of 114 per-technique kappas, **random 10% sample, 195 sentences, phase (ii)** |
| src-0021 | 0.76 | single kappa over all sentence-level TTPrint-Bench annotations |
| src-0021 | 0.74 | pairwise kappa over the **adjudication-selected contested subset** of TRAM-Clean |

The "0.68–0.76 band" framing is therefore **worse off than ctr-0015 left it**, and the phrase
"two teams, two corpora, one conclusion" must not be used. The underlying qualitative point —
that trained annotators on parent-level ATT&CK labelling agree substantially but well short of
the near-perfect agreement an exact-ID scoring rule presumes — survives, and now rests most
strongly on the 981/2,076 relabelling figure rather than on any of the three kappa values.
The standing prohibition from `ttp-attack-mapping-reliability` open_questions[1] is undisturbed
and reinforced: **an agreement statistic is not an accuracy baseline.**

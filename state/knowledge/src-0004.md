## Summary

Heise Online (citing Der Spiegel's original reporting) documents a concrete, real-world
failure case of AI-generated content contaminating an official cyber threat
intelligence publication: ENISA's (EU Agency for Cybersecurity) "Threat Landscape 2025"
report. Researchers at Westfälische Hochschule noticed that some passages read as
oddly inconclusive and that several cited source links did not work; on closer
inspection they found strong indications of AI-hallucinated citations, including a
telltale sign — a passage referencing Microsoft's name for the threat actor APT29,
even though Microsoft's own naming convention for that group is "Midnight Blizzard"
(not "APT29"), consistent with an LLM conflating/hallucinating naming conventions
rather than a human author's error. This is a directly on-topic, named, dated incident
of unreliable AI-generated CTI reaching production/publication at a major institutional
source.

## Key claims

1. Two ENISA reports (published October and November 2025) contained AI-hallucinated
   citations; in one report, 26 of 492 footnotes were incorrect, discovered when
   researchers found that clicking through many of the cited links did not resolve to
   real sources.
2. A specific, checkable hallucination signature was found: content attributed a
   non-Microsoft naming convention ("APT29") to Microsoft in a way inconsistent with
   Microsoft's actual naming practice (Microsoft calls this group "Midnight
   Blizzard"), which the researchers treated as a technical fingerprint of LLM-authored
   text rather than a simple human citation error.
3. ENISA's official response characterized the errors as "human errors" and confirmed
   that AI was permitted to make "minor editorial revisions" to the report — i.e., the
   agency acknowledged AI involvement post-hoc but did not fully attribute the citation
   fabrications to the AI process. The report was subsequently revised (per related
   reporting, an updated version was posted 2026-01-09 to fix some links).

## Relevant quotes

- Professor Christian Dietrich (Westfälische Hochschule): "All it would have taken was
  one click" to verify the sources before publication.
- Chaos Computer Club spokesperson Linus Neumann called the incident "embarrassing,"
  noting ENISA is Europe's central authority for cybersecurity expertise and standards.
- ENISA attributed the errors to "human errors" and stated AI was permitted for "minor
  editorial revisions."

# Mapping CVEs to MITRE ATT&CK Techniques (arXiv 2607.25572)

## Why this source was accepted, and what it is NOT

Collected at cycle 125 (a T3 targeting `task-dependent-reliability-framing`) against that issue's
**`open_questions[1]`** — *does human-in-the-loop validation specifically transfer to
reasoning-heavy tasks, and if so how much of the IoC-extraction gain would it recover?* That
question's negative finding was last refreshed at **cycle 16**, 109 cycles before this one.

**READ THIS BEFORE YOU CITE IT. IT DOES NOT ANSWER `open_questions[1]` AS ASKED.** The question asks
about **human-in-the-loop validation at inference time**, in the sense of src-0003's LANCE pipeline
where an analyst confirms or corrects each model-proposed label in the deployed workflow. This paper
measures something adjacent but **distinct**: whether **LLM-generated labels can substitute for
expert-curated labels in the TRAINING SET** of a supervised classifier. The human element it varies
is **label provenance for training data**, not analyst review of outputs. A successor must not
report this as a HITL-at-inference result. What it does supply is the *converse* measurement —
evidence about how load-bearing the human-curated component is on a reasoning-heavy CTI task — and
that is why it is entered here.

**Task and input modality.** CVE **free-text descriptions** → MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise techniques,
multi-label. **NOTE THE MODALITY GAP, IT IS LOAD-BEARING:** every other ATT&CK measurement in this
base (src-0002 CTI-ATE, src-0007, src-0020, src-0021, src-0023) maps from **threat reports or
narrative CTI text**, not from CVE descriptions. This is a **sixth** measurement unit for ATT&CK
extraction and it is not convertible to the other five — it corroborates rather than conflicts with
`ttp-attack-mapping-reliability`'s supported candidate that ATT&CK-extraction reliability is
reported in mutually unconvertible units.

## What the study does

- **Gold dataset:** 1,207 CVEs from expert **MITRE Center for Threat-Informed Defense (CTID)**
  mappings. Fixed splits: **1,086 train / 121 test** (body render).
- **Model:** multi-label classifier, `roberta-base` fine-tuned throughout; a robustness check on
  `ModernBERT-base` is reported (body render).
- **Baseline:** zero-shot embedding-similarity.
- **Comparison arm rejected up front:** the CWE→CAPEC→ATT&CK derivation chain, "whose
  table-expansion artifacts we quantify" (abstract, both renders).
- **LLM expansion arm:** an LLM labeler (body render: `qwen3.5:122b`, assertive prompt, single call)
  labels independently sampled CVEs; nested expansion subsets of **100, 300, 600, 984** additional
  CVEs, **five random seeds per configuration**.
- **Metrics:** Recall@k (k ∈ {3, 5, 10}), MRR, Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 at a 0.5 threshold; Micro-F1
  also used as the LLM-versus-gold agreement measure.

## Key claims (verbatim-supported)

**KC1 — the curated-gold classifier, and the corrected protocol. CONFIRMED ON BOTH RENDERS.**
From the abstract, verbatim:

> "we train a multi-label classifier on a curated gold dataset of 1,207 CVEs from expert MITRE
> Center for Threat-Informed Defense mappings. The resulting model approximately doubles recall@5
> compared with a zero-shot embedding-similarity baseline and improves every ranking metric."

> "Using a corrected protocol based on validation-split checkpoint selection, the gold-only model
> achieves recall@5 of (0.673 ± 0.019)"

**KC2 — the null result on LLM-generated labels, which is the paper's headline. CONFIRMED ON BOTH
RENDERS.** From the abstract, verbatim:

> "LLM-generated labels, with approximately 0.39 agreement with expert annotations, provide no
> reliable improvement at any expansion size and reduce rare-technique coverage at around 1,000
> added CVEs"

> "A final scaling study shows that additional expert-curated data consistently improves
> performance, whereas LLM-labeled data does not, indicating that the classifier is limited by label
> quality rather than dataset size."

**KC3 — the evaluation-artefact finding, which is methodologically the most transferable part of
this paper. CONFIRMED ON BOTH RENDERS.** From the abstract, verbatim:

> "Initial experiments suggest contradictory conclusions: a single run indicates degraded
> performance, while averaging over five random seeds suggests a small gain. However, an independent
> replication and an expansion-size study (100--984 additional CVEs) show that the apparent
> improvement is an evaluation artifact. ... The root cause is evaluation noise. Selecting
> checkpoints on a small test split effectively maximizes over many noisy evaluations, producing
> recall@5 differences of up to 0.05 between otherwise identical runs."

**KC4 — the authors' inherent-task-ceiling argument. SINGLE RENDER (body) ONLY, AND IT IS AN
INFERENCE OF THE AUTHORS', NOT A MEASUREMENT.** The body render returns, under the paper's own
limitations, that "Mapping a CVE to ATT&CK is genuinely hard and partly subjective" and that both
the classifier and the LLM reach similar agreement levels against gold (~0.39–0.41), which the
authors read as "suggesting an inherent task ceiling rather than modelling failure".
**DO NOT UPGRADE THIS INTO AN INTER-ANNOTATOR AGREEMENT FIGURE.** 0.39/0.41 are *model-versus-gold*
agreements. **No human-versus-human agreement figure is reported on either render.** The ceiling
claim is therefore the authors' interpretation of two model-vs-gold numbers, not a measured bound on
the task, and it must be cited as such.

## CITATION HOLD — ONE FIGURE IS DISPUTED BETWEEN THE RENDERS AND MAY NOT BE QUOTED

**THE MACRO-F1 DECREMENT AT THE LARGEST EXPANSION SIZE IS REPORTED AS TWO DIFFERENT NUMBERS.** The
abstract render states the decline as **0.04**, verbatim: "reduce rare-technique coverage at around
1,000 added CVEs (macro-F1 decreases by 0.04)". The body render instead returns gold-only
0.177 ± 0.014 against gold+984-LLM-rows 0.151 ± 0.014, a decline of **0.026**. These cannot both be
the same quantity stated to the same precision. **NO CYCLE MAY QUOTE EITHER MACRO-F1 DECREMENT** —
nor the 0.177 / 0.151 pair, nor the 0.04 — until the paper's own tables have been read directly and
the two referents separated (they may be different protocols, the abstract's possibly pre-correction
and the body's post-correction, but that is a hypothesis and cycle 125 did not test it).
**THE DIRECTION AND THE NULL VERDICT ARE NOT UNDER THE HOLD** and are confirmed on both renders: no
reliable improvement at any expansion size, and reduced rare-technique coverage at the largest size.

**Also single-render (body only), therefore weaker and flagged:** the Recall@5 expansion pair
0.673 ± 0.019 → 0.651 ± 0.022; the agreement figures 0.392 (LLM vs gold) and 0.407 (classifier vs
gold); the split sizes 1,086/121; the model names; the "validate-before-expand" protocol name. Each
of `0.392`, `0.407`, `0.651`, `0.177`, `0.151` was checked against the abstract render explicitly
and returned **ABSENT**. The gold-only 0.673 ± 0.019 IS on both renders.

## Limitations — charged, not credited

1. **arXiv preprint, no peer-review venue confirmed.** The body render states submission to
   hack.lu 2026; the abstract render does not contain the string `hack.lu`. Weaker provenance than
   the venue-confirmed sources in this base.
2. **AUTHOR-COUNT DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN RENDERS, RECORDED AND NOT RESOLVED.** Body render: two
   authors, *Cédric Bonhomme, Alexandre Dulaunoy*. Abstract render: *Alexandre Dulaunoy* only.
   **This is the SAME failure pattern already recorded at src-0042 limitation 2**, on a different
   paper and a different arXiv id, which makes it a property of the render pair rather than of
   either paper. Neither render outranks the other; a cycle needing the author list must re-derive
   it (dblp / OpenAlex).
3. **DATE DISAGREEMENT.** Body 2026-07-23; abstract render "Tue, 28 Jul 2026". Unresolved.
4. **Gold-set size and selection bias, stated by the authors** (body render): "The gold set is small
   (~1,200 CVEs) and not a representative sample of the CVE corpus. Both CTID sources
   over-represent vulnerabilities exploited in the wild...Models trained here will inherit that
   skew."
5. **Ground-truth scarcity, stated by the authors** (body render): "Finding a usable ground-truth
   dataset is itself a central difficulty of this task...Even expert mappings are not automatically
   exhaustive."
6. **ATT&CK version drift, stated by the authors** (body render): the 2021 CTID mappings predate
   current ATT&CK versions, and identifier normalisation "cannot infer modern sub-techniques from
   older labels". **This is the same parent/sub-technique hazard this base already documented in
   src-0017's loader (ctr-0009, ttp candidate 3), arriving from a different direction.**
7. **Expansion-result scope, stated by the authors** (body render): "Established for one local model
   family, one prompt configuration, expansion sizes up to 984 CVEs, and five seeds per
   configuration...What it rules out is the hope that scale alone rescues labels at this agreement
   level." **The null is therefore about labels AT ~0.39 AGREEMENT, not about LLM labelling in
   general** — a better labeler is not tested and is not ruled out.
8. **One base encoder** (`roberta-base`), no search over encoders; a `ModernBERT-base` robustness
   check is reported to replicate the macro-F1 degradation (body render, single render).
9. **Residual optimism disclosed by the authors** (body render): absolute numbers under the
   "pre-correction protocol" carry "mild select-on-test optimism"; corrected re-runs confirm the
   null verdict but produce roughly 1 point lower recall.
10. **NO HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP-AT-INFERENCE ARM.** No analyst-review condition of the src-0003 kind is
    measured, so this source **cannot** quantify how much of an IoC-extraction-style HITL gain would
    be recovered on a reasoning-heavy task. `open_questions[1]` is narrowed by this source, **not
    closed**.

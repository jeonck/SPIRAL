# Restoring Calibration for Aligned LLMs (arXiv 2505.01997, ICML 2025)

## Why this source was accepted, and exactly which question it is for

Collected at cycle 134 (a T1 targeting `consistency-calibration-as-failure-mode`) against that
issue's **`open_questions[11]`**, which asks: *does src-0001's finding that fine-tuning can WORSEN
CALIBRATION (CVE-generation ECE 0.15 → 0.91) replicate ANYWHERE, in ANY domain?* That question is
**explicitly domain-unrestricted** — it says an out-of-domain fine-tuning-versus-proper-score study
counts — and it recorded **ZERO SOURCES** before this cycle. This source is the first entry in the
base other than src-0001 that pairs a **training intervention** with a **proper-scoring calibration
metric** and reports numbers.

**IT IS NOT A CTI SOURCE AND MAY NEVER BE COUNTED TOWARD CTI MULTI-SOURCE SUPPORT.** Cyber threat
intelligence, IoC extraction, MITRE ATT&CK and threat reports are all absent from it. It falls under
the same standing exclusion rule already recorded for src-0013, src-0028, src-0029 and src-0042. It
does **NOT** answer `open_questions[5]` (a second *CTI* calibration source) and must not be proposed
for it.

**ELICITATION MODALITY, RECORDED VERBATIM AT SOURCE AS ctr-0027 REQUIRES.** Confidence is
**token-probability-derived, not verbalized** — the *same* modality as src-0001, which is what makes
the comparison to src-0001 meaningful at all. From the body render, Section 3, Equation 1, quoted on
two renders identically (mathematical notation transliterated to ASCII):

> "The confidence of LLMs in option j is defined as: p_j(x) = pi_theta(y = j|x) / sum over j' in
> {A, B, C, D} of pi_theta(y = j'|x)."

Note the summation index set `{A, B, C, D}`: **this is a four-option multiple-choice confidence**,
which bounds how far the numbers transfer. See Limitations.

## What the study does

- **Intervention studied:** preference alignment (DPO and RLHF), i.e. *alignment* fine-tuning — see
  the scope warning below, this is **not** the same intervention as src-0001's supervised
  domain fine-tuning.
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-Tulu-8B (DPO), Vicuna-7B-v1.5 (RLHF), OLMo 2-7B (DPO), Mistral-7B (DPO).
  **NO MODEL OVERLAP with src-0001** (gpt4o, gemini-1.5-pro-latest, mistral-large-2) — Mistral-7B is
  a different model from mistral-large-2 and must not be treated as a replication on one.
- **Datasets:** MMLU, MedMCQA, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard,
  UltraFeedback-Binarized-Preferences.
- **Metrics:** two ECE variants, reported as `conf-ECE` and `cw-ECE`. **No Brier score is reported**
  (ABSENT on both renders).
- **Methods proposed:** CFT (calibration-aware fine-tuning) for the "calibratable" regime and RCFT
  (an EM-algorithm-based ECE regularization of the fine-tuning loss) for the "non-calibratable"
  regime, compared against temperature scaling and label smoothing.

## Key claims (verbatim-supported)

**KC1 — alignment fine-tuning degrades calibration; this is the paper's own framing. CONFIRMED ON
BOTH RENDERS.** From the abstract, verbatim:

> "One of the key technologies for the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) is preference
> alignment. However, a notable side effect of preference alignment is poor calibration: while the
> pre-trained models are typically well-calibrated, LLMs tend to become poorly calibrated after
> alignment with human preferences."

and

> "we observe that the preference collapse issue in alignment undesirably generalizes to the
> calibration scenario, causing LLMs to exhibit overconfidence and poor calibration."

**THE DIRECTION MATCHES src-0001. THE MAGNITUDE OF THE "BEFORE" SIDE IS NOT AVAILABLE HERE** — see
Limitation (1): **no ECE number for the pre-trained baseline is printed on either render.** The
before/after is asserted qualitatively; only the *after* side is quantified. A successor must not
report this as a measured pre-versus-post-alignment ECE delta.

**KC2 — the quantified post-alignment ECE levels, and the recovery range. CONFIRMED ON BOTH RENDERS,
CELL FOR CELL.** Table 2 (caption, body render: *"Performance comparison among DPO/RLHF, Temperature
Scaling, Label Smoothing, CFT, and RCFT across four models (Llama3.1-8B-Tulu, Vicuna-7B, Olmo2-7B,
and Mistral-7B) in in-domain and out-domain scenarios."*), in-domain column:

| model | alignment | conf-ECE | cw-ECE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Llama3.1-8B-Tulu | DPO | 0.1953 | 0.0953 |
| Vicuna-7B | RLHF | 0.1422 | 0.0979 |
| Olmo2-7B | DPO | 0.1555 | 0.0873 |
| Mistral-7B | DPO | 0.2010 | 0.0909 |

and, verbatim on both renders:

> "Our approach reduces the ECE of preference-aligned models from 14.22%–20.10% to 2.39%–6.51%
> across different model architectures and alignment approaches, which validates the effectiveness
> of the proposed methods."

**NOTE THE ARITHMETIC BEFORE YOU USE THE RANGE:** 14.22%–20.10% is exactly the conf-ECE column
above (0.1422 minimum at Vicuna-7B, 0.2010 maximum at Mistral-7B). It is a **conf-ECE** range, not a
cw-ECE range and not a pooled one. The 2.39%–6.51% figures are **post-intervention** values for the
authors' own methods and are **NOT** printed as a separate column I read; they are stated only in
that sentence. Quote the sentence, do not decompose it per-model.

**KC3 — the two-regime claim, which is the paper's structural contribution. CONFIRMED ON BOTH
RENDERS.** From the abstract, verbatim:

> "we categorize models into two regimes: calibratable and non-calibratable, defined by bounds of
> Expected Calibration Error (ECE). In the calibratable regime, we propose a calibration-aware
> fine-tuning approach to achieve proper calibration without compromising LLMs' performance.
> However, as models are further fine-tuned for better performance, they enter the non-calibratable
> regime."

**THIS IS THE MOST TRANSFERABLE IDEA IN THE SOURCE FOR THIS BASE'S PURPOSES:** it asserts that
pushing fine-tuning further *for accuracy* moves a model into a regime where low calibration error
is no longer attainable without an explicit regularizer. That is a mechanism under which
src-0001's ECE 0.15 → 0.91 is not an anomaly. **IT IS THE AUTHORS' CLAIM, NOT A REPLICATION OF
src-0001**, and it is made on multiple-choice benchmarks, not on CTI generation.

## THE RENDER DISAGREEMENT, AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING — READ BEFORE CITING THE DOMAIN-FINE-TUNING LIMB

The **abstract** states, verbatim (abstract render, direct from arxiv.org/abs/2505.01997):

> "To address this, we demonstrate the importance of fine-tuning with domain-specific knowledge to
> alleviate the overconfidence issue."

Asked directly for that sentence and for any measured ECE change attributable to domain fine-tuning
alone, the **body render answered ABSENT** on both limbs: it could locate no body sentence stating
that domain-specific fine-tuning alleviates overconfidence, and **no quantified domain-fine-tuning
ECE comparison anywhere.** So the claim exists at abstract level and this cycle could not tie it to
a number.

**WHY THIS MATTERS:** taken at face value the abstract sentence points the *opposite* way from
src-0001, where domain fine-tuning on CTI data drove ECE from 0.15 to 0.91 on CVE generation and
worsened ECE on 7 of 9 Table 6 rows. Cycle 134 opened **ctr-0068** on exactly this and scoped it to
what the evidence actually supports: a measured result on one side against an unquantified
abstract-level assertion on the other. **DO NOT CITE THIS SOURCE AS MEASURED EVIDENCE THAT DOMAIN
FINE-TUNING IMPROVES CALIBRATION.** Cite the sentence, label it abstract-only and unquantified.

## Limitations (stated by the authors unless marked)

1. **NO PRE-ALIGNMENT ECE NUMBER IS PRINTED** (agent finding, both renders). The "pre-trained models
   are typically well-calibrated" premise is asserted, not tabulated here. This is the single
   largest limit on using this source as a before/after replication.
2. From Section 7, "Limitation and Future Work", verbatim: *"Current study focuses on
   multiple-choice settings; extending ... to free-form generation is important"*. **src-0001's
   worst calibration failures are on GENERATION sub-tasks**, which is precisely the setting this
   paper excludes. The transfer is therefore not clean and must not be asserted.
3. Also from Section 7: determining which regime a model falls into "reduces to understanding ...
   accuracy threshold" and remains open; *"Determining the constant C in Theorem 4.5 is nontrivial"*;
   calibration where an LLM "is initially poorly calibrated" is unexplored; quantized and black-box
   API models are unexplored.
4. **THE ECE-AS-ESTIMATOR IMPEACHMENT IN THIS BASE APPLIES HERE TOO.** Per src-0028 and src-0029
   (already in the base, and already applied to src-0001 in `candidate_resolutions[1]`), a
   fixed-binning ECE is a lower bound on the calibration error and is bin-count-dependent. **This
   paper's bin count and binning scheme were not captured by either render this cycle** — treat that
   as an open item, not as an absence.
5. **Intervention mismatch, agent finding, and it is the reason this is not a clean replication:**
   src-0001 fine-tunes on *task data* (supervised, CTI reports). This paper's degradation finding is
   about *preference alignment* (DPO/RLHF). Both are "fine-tuning" in ordinary usage and both are
   paired with ECE, which is why this source answers `open_questions[11]` at all — but they are not
   the same intervention and the question's core is only **partially** discharged.

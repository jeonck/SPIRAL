## Summary

CyberSOCEval (arXiv 2509.20166) is an industry-originated benchmark (large author list
including senior authors from CrowdStrike, e.g. Sven Krasser, and Meta, e.g. Joshua
Saxe), released as part of Meta's open-source "CyberSecEval 4" suite. It targets two
SOC-defender-relevant domains the authors say are under-covered by existing
benchmarks: malware analysis and threat-intelligence reasoning. Unlike src-0002
(CTIBench, mid-2024 models), this benchmark evaluates newer/larger LLMs including
reasoning models with test-time scaling, giving a more current (2025-era) data point on
whether model scaling is closing the CTI reliability gap.

## Key claims

1. Larger/more modern LLMs perform better on CyberSOCEval's malware-analysis and
   threat-intelligence-reasoning tasks, "confirming the training scaling laws
   paradigm" — i.e. raw scale continues to help on these tasks as of the 2025 model
   generation tested.
2. Reasoning models using test-time scaling do NOT get the same performance boost on
   these CTI/malware tasks that they get on coding and math benchmarks — suggesting
   generic "reasoning" post-training does not transfer well to cybersecurity threat
   analysis, and that domain-specific training/data is likely still necessary.
3. "Current LLMs are far from saturating our evaluations," i.e. as of this benchmark's
   publication, no evaluated model is near a ceiling — there remains substantial
   measured headroom/unreliability in both malware analysis and threat-intel reasoning
   tasks industry-wide, not just in academic benchmarks like CTIBench.

## Relevant quotes

- "core defensive domains with inadequate coverage in current benchmarks" (stated
  motivation for building CyberSOCEval).
- "larger, more modern LLMs tend to perform better, confirming the training scaling
  laws paradigm."
- "reasoning models leveraging test time scaling do not achieve the same boost as in
  coding and math."
- "Current LLMs are far from saturating our evaluations."

## Limitations of this source

- Retrieved via automated abstract-page summarization; exact model names and
  per-model/per-task numeric scores were not captured in this pass and should be
  pulled from the full paper before citing specific figures.
- Industry-affiliated authorship (CrowdStrike, Meta) — the benchmark's task design and
  framing may reflect these organizations' commercial priorities (e.g. SOC tooling
  relevant to CrowdStrike's product line); treat comparative claims about specific
  vendors' models with that in mind.
- As with CTIBench (src-0002), this is a benchmark snapshot tied to the specific model
  set evaluated at publication time (2025); results will age as new model generations
  release.

## Summary

arXiv 2605.24171v1 [cs.LG], 22 May 2026. Authors: Steffen J. Camarato, Yahya Hmaiti,
Mandana Ghadamian, David Mohaisen — all University of Central Florida. No overlap with
src-0001's team or any other team in this base.

Why it was added (cycle 15, T1 targeting `consistency-calibration-as-failure-mode`):
src-0001 measured *consistency* by re-prompting each input 10 times and reporting
bootstrapped CI widths. Until now no other source in this base measured output stability
under repeated or varied prompting at all. PromptAudit is a controlled study of exactly
that axis on a security task — it fixes dataset, decoding and parsing and varies ONLY
the prompting strategy, across 5 strategies x 5 open-weight models x 1,000 CVEs
(6,074 code samples, 16 languages). It also measures **abstention**, which the target
issue explicitly names as a qualifying axis and which no prior source in this base
quantifies.

Its most useful property for this issue is that it separates two things src-0001 leaves
entangled: how much of the instability is the *model* and how much is the *prompt*.
PromptAudit's answer is that it is jointly determined and that the model-to-model spread
in prompt sensitivity is itself large — one model's F1 moves 0.398 across strategies
while another's moves 0.103.

## Key claims

1. **Output instability under prompt variation is large and highly model-dependent.**
   Gemma exhibits the largest cross-strategy F1 range, 0.398, spanning F1 0.102 under
   adaptive chain-of-thought to 0.499 under self-consistency; Mistral is the stability
   baseline with the smallest cross-strategy F1 range, 0.103. A ~4x spread in
   prompt-sensitivity between two models evaluated on identical data means "how
   consistent is an LLM on this security task" has no single answer independent of which
   model is asked.
2. **An intervention widely assumed to help can sharply degrade performance — an
   independent instance of the pattern src-0001 found for fine-tuning and few-shot.**
   "The A-CoT template reduces recall relative to CoT for every model, with the largest
   drops in CodeLlama (0.685 → 0.279), Falcon (0.541 → 0.223), Gemma (0.420 → 0.057),
   and Mistral (0.423 → 0.280)." Gemma's recall falls by roughly 86% relative.
3. **Self-consistency buys agreement by refusing to answer, and the abstention cost is
   severe and uneven.** When sampled outputs disagree, coverage drops to 51.00%
   (CodeLlama), 48.93% (Mistral), 60.85% (Gemma), 39.04% (DeepSeek) and 24.04% (Falcon)
   — i.e. one model declines to produce an answer on roughly three quarters of inputs.
   By contrast the plain CoT template achieves the highest mean F1 (0.465) and mean
   effective F1 (0.427) at a mean abstention rate of 7.28%. This is the first measured
   abstention/selective-prediction data in this knowledge base and it establishes that
   consistency and coverage trade off directly rather than both improving together.

## Relevant quotes

Abstract, verbatim, from https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24171:

> "Large language models are increasingly used for vulnerability detection, yet their
> reliability under different prompt formulations remains uncharacterized. We present
> PromptAudit, a controlled evaluation framework that isolates prompt effects by fixing
> the dataset, decoding, and parsing while varying only the prompting strategy. Using
> five prompting strategies across five open-weight models on 1,000 CVEs (6,074 code
> samples spanning 16 programming languages), we evaluate accuracy, recall, abstention,
> coverage, and effective F1. We find that standard chain-of-thought prompting achieves
> the strongest overall operational performance, while few-shot prompting provides
> model-dependent benefits that are most pronounced for prompt-sensitive models. In
> contrast, adaptive chain-of-thought frequently suppresses recall and self-consistency
> induces excessive abstention, sharply reducing effective performance. These results
> show that vulnerability detection behavior is jointly determined by the model and the
> prompt, and that prompt sensitivity is a first-class system property that must be
> explicitly characterized in evaluation and deployment."

On cross-strategy variation (body):

> "Gemma serves as the prompt-sensitivity baseline: it exhibits the largest
> cross-strategy F1 range (0.398), with standard F1 spanning from 0.102 under A-CoT to
> 0.499 under self-consistency."

> "Mistral serves as the stability baseline: it achieves the smallest cross-strategy F1
> range (0.103), indicating low sensitivity to prompt formulation."

On the CoT baseline:

> "The evaluated CoT template achieves the highest mean F1 (0.465) and mean effective F1
> (0.427), with a low mean abstention rate of 7.28%."

On adaptive-CoT recall collapse:

> "The A-CoT template reduces recall relative to CoT for every model, with the largest
> drops in CodeLlama (0.685 → 0.279), Falcon (0.541 → 0.223), Gemma (0.420 → 0.057), and
> Mistral (0.423 → 0.280)."

On self-consistency abstention:

> "Under our A-CoT-based self-consistency, coverage drops across all models when sampled
> outputs disagree, falling to 51.00% for CodeLlama, 48.93% for Mistral, 60.85% for
> Gemma, 39.04% for DeepSeek, and 24.04% for Falcon."

## Limitations of this source

- **SCOPE GAP — NOT CTI.** The task is vulnerability detection over CVE code samples,
  not extraction or reasoning over narrative threat reports. Admitted under the same
  standard as src-0008 and src-0013, with the gap recorded here so no downstream claim
  can quietly generalise it to threat intelligence.
- **IT MEASURES PROMPT SENSITIVITY, NOT REPEAT-QUERY CONSISTENCY, AND THE TWO ARE NOT
  THE SAME AXIS.** src-0001 re-prompted the *identical* input 10 times and reported CI
  widths over that. PromptAudit varies the prompting *strategy* between conditions.
  Both are forms of output instability and the paper is a genuine second measurement of
  the general phenomenon, but it is NOT a replication of src-0001's method and its F1
  ranges are NOT comparable to src-0001's bootstrapped CI widths (0.02 extraction /
  0.06 generation). For same-input repetition see src-0016 instead.
- **Open-weight models only**, and none overlap with src-0001's, src-0002's or
  src-0005's lists (CodeLlama, Falcon, Gemma, Mistral, DeepSeek). It does not answer
  open_question (ii). Note it does contain "Mistral", but src-0001 tested
  mistral-large-2 specifically and no version is matched here from the fetched text.
- **No ECE, no Brier, no reliability diagram.** This source speaks to the consistency
  and abstention halves of the issue, not the calibration half. src-0013 supplies the
  calibration half. Neither supplies both, which is worth stating plainly: the base
  still has no non-src-0001 source measuring consistency AND calibration together.
- Retrieved by a single automated fetch of the arXiv HTML. The abstract is
  verbatim-confirmed. The numeric findings were returned as quoted body sentences, not
  as a pulled table; **the results tables have not been pulled in full** and a future
  G2 should do so before any of these numbers is treated as load-bearing.
- v1 preprint, no venue acceptance stated as of access date; it has not been through
  peer review, unlike src-0013 (ICSME 2026).

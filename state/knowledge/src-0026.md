## Summary

The second primary artefact of the src-0004 incident, read for the first time at cycle 72,
and **the only one of the two recovered in full**. This is the corrected PDF of the report
ENISA published 6 November 2025 and revised 09 January 2026 — a revision src-0010's landing
page describes as "updated to correct some broken links and typos".

The render self-reported **NOT TRUNCATED** and returned the document end to end, so
absences established on it are **whole-document** absences rather than partial ones. That
is what distinguishes it from src-0025, whose render truncated.

## Access constraint

Identical to src-0025: a direct fetch returns PDF binary and must be routed through a
text-extraction proxy (`r.jina.ai`); the local `Read` tool cannot render PDFs here
(poppler-utils absent). The stored URL is the **direct ENISA URL** and resolves
(HTTP 200, 1.6 MB).

## Key claims

1. **No AI disclosure anywhere in the document.** Asked directly whether any part mentions
   AI, artificial intelligence, generative AI, LLM or large language model *in connection
   with how this report was produced, written or edited* — as distinct from AI as a threat
   topic — the render answered **ABSENT**, on a render that self-reported NOT TRUNCATED.
   This is the **strongest form this absence has ever taken in this base**: cycles 12, 34
   and 56 established it for the *landing page* only (src-0010), and
   `institutional-incident-real-world-impact` open_questions[1] expressly flagged that the
   PDF had not been pulled. **For this report that gap is now closed.**
2. **No internal revision notice.** Asked separately for any revision notice, version
   history or changelog *inside* the document, the render answered **ABSENT**. The
   correction is recorded on ENISA's landing page and **not in the artefact itself**.
   (Contrast src-0025, which does carry a `LOG HISTORY` section.)
3. **Exactly 149 numbered footnotes**, the highest being 149. This count **rules this
   report out** as the subject of src-0004's 26-of-492 figure — an ambiguity src-0004
   leaves open and this base has carried since cycle 1.
4. **The Legal Notice and Acknowledgements are word-for-word identical to those of the
   pre-correction v1.1 (src-0027).** Consequence: the January 2026 correction added **no
   disclosure, no erratum, no cause statement and no change of attribution** to the
   artefact.

## Limitations of this source

- Note the small wording difference from src-0025's Legal Notice ("unless otherwise
  stated" / "must mention ENISA as its source" here, against "unless stated otherwise" /
  "must contain ENISA as its source" there). The two reports carry **near-identical but
  not byte-identical** boilerplate; both are quoted as printed and neither has been
  normalised. The clause this project relies on — external-website liability — is
  **identical** in both.
- The AI absence is established on **one URL form and one render**, though on an untruncated
  one. Per rule (v) it is not confirmed on an independent second form.
- Like src-0009 and src-0010, this artefact assigns **no cause** to the link defects and
  gives **no count** of incorrect footnotes. It neither supports nor contradicts src-0004's
  AI attribution; it is silent. The AI-attribution limb of the ENISA incident remains
  **single-sourced to src-0004**.
- The filename says "TL 2024" while the report is dated November 2025. This is ENISA's own
  filename oddity, already recorded on src-0010 at cycle 12; it is recorded here as
  observed and is not evidence of anything.

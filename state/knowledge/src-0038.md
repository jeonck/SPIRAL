# Summary

Single-author preprint (Samar Ansari, School of Computing and Engineering Sciences, University of
Chester, UK), submitted **5 February 2026**. It builds a five-category failure-mode taxonomy over
the 100 hallucinated citations that GPTZero found in NeurIPS 2025 accepted papers.

**WHY THIS SOURCE WAS COLLECTED, AND IT IS NOT THE TAXONOMY.** It was collected at cycle 101 for
`incident-evidence-vendor-concentration` because it is the **only artefact this base has ever held
that states a GPTZero sweep DENOMINATOR**. Verbatim, via the r.jina.ai render of the PDF:

> "GPTZero 4, a Canadian AI detection company, used their automated Hallucination Check tool to
> scan 4,841 (of the 5290 papers accepted) papers from NeurIPS 2025."

> "GPTZero's analysis identified 100 hallucinated citations spanning 53 distinct papers
> (representing 1% of all accepted papers)."

The abstract on the `abs` page independently reproduces the numerator and rate: "appearing in 53
published papers (approx. 1% of all accepted papers)". **The 53 and the ~1% are therefore
two-form confirmed** (r.jina.ai render of the PDF, and the arXiv abstract page, fetched
separately). The 4,841 / 5,290 denominator pair is **one-form** (PDF render only) and should be
treated as provisional until re-rendered.

**A SEARCH-TOOL FIGURE THAT IS NOT ENTERED AND MUST NOT BE CITED.** The web-search summary that
surfaced this paper asserted "100 hallucinated citations across **51** different publications".
The paper itself says **53**, twice, at two independent renders. Per this loop's standing query
discipline an enumerated count returned by the search tool is not evidence in either direction;
**51 is recorded here only as a warning and is not a claim of this base.**

# Provenance — this is NOT an independent replication

Stated plainly because it is the thing a future cycle is most likely to get wrong. Ansari did
**not** re-resolve any citation independently. Verbatim:

> "For our analysis, we used the complete dataset of 100 verified hallucinations as documented in
> GPTZero's public report [1], which included the full citation text, associated paper title, and
> diagnostic notes explaining why each citation was classified as a hallucination."

> "The author thanks the GPTZero team for making their NeurIPS 2025 hallucination analysis
> publicly available."

So this paper is **downstream of GPTZero**, in the same way `src-0012`, `src-0035` and `src-0036`
are. It is a fourth instance of that structure and the most probative one, because an academic
preprint is precisely where independent re-resolution would be expected and it did not happen.

It does, however, corroborate the **human-verification step** that cycle 90 established for the EY
instalment, now for the NeurIPS sweep, verbatim:

> "Each flagged citation was subsequently verified by human experts at GPTZero to confirm it
> represented a probable hallucination rather than a legitimate archival source or temporary
> indexing gap."

# Key figures from the taxonomy (secondary to the above)

Total Fabrication 66%, Partial Attribute Corruption 27%, Identifier Hijacking 4%, Placeholder
Hallucination 2%, Semantic Hallucination 1%; every hallucination (100%) exhibited compound failure
modes; 92% of contaminated papers contain 1–2 hallucinations, 8% contain 4–13. These are quoted
from the arXiv abstract. They describe the **shape** of hallucinated citations, not their rate.

The abstract also states the problem "extends beyond NeurIPS to other major conferences,
government reports, and professional consulting" — an authorial assertion, **unquantified**, and
it must not be cited as a measurement.

# Limitations

- The denominator figure is **reported at second hand**: Ansari states what GPTZero scanned. This
  base has not seen GPTZero's own NeurIPS report. That is one remove, and it is why the entry
  below says the denominator is *published somewhere* rather than *published by the vendor in a
  form this base has verified at source*.
- The corpus is **academic conference papers**, not consulting or CTI reports. Nothing here
  transfers to the corporate-report population.
- Single author, preprint, not peer-reviewed at time of access.
